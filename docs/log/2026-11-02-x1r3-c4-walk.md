**What.** When C4's metadata carries a verdict (GateMem), its read walks the core pipeline's ranking of every listed item (`walkToAdmitted`) until it has passed as many allowed items as C5's read returns, and shows every item walked past, allowed and denied, each labelled. C2 and C3, and MEM-INV-Bench's C4, keep the plain top 20.

**Why.** Round 2's C4 showed C2's top 20 with verdicts, so its allowed items were a strict subset of C5's list on 2,026 of 2,218 checkpoints, and C4 minus C5 mixed retrieval with mechanism (review round 3, V-2). The orchestrator chose the walk over C5 cutting before its filter and over declaring the asymmetry C5's mechanism.

**Result.** C4's allowed items equal C5's list in order on every fixture checkpoint and query, and on all 2,218 checkpoints; every denied item ranks above the last allowed one.

**Consequence.** ADR 0031 decision 4 rewritten, still proposed.
