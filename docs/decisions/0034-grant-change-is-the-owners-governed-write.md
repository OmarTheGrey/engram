# ADR 0034: A grant change is the owner's governed write: one update event, a new version in place

Status: accepted, 2026-11-25, by the lead ("accept the adrs"); proposed 2026-11-19
Deciders: drafted by the orchestrating session's plan (docs/tasks/key-free-work-plan.md); accepted by the lead on 2026-11-25, with every amendment it carries and the answers to items 34 and 35 of `docs/tasks/lead-decisions.md`
Supersedes: nothing; it gives ADR 0021's consent grants the operation card E9.4's acceptance ("grant change recorded as an event") needs.

## Context

Grants live on each item (`Grant` in `packages/core/src/schema.ts`: `to`, `class`, `mode`, `expires`). The write gate checks every grant an item carries (ADR 0021, decision D4: a principal or a prefixed pattern, a class and an expiry, none on model or compaction content), and the read filter honours a live one. The twin profile (card E9.2) has no default grants. Until now no operation changed an item's grants: only a rewrite of the same item id through `Plane.write`, which any member of the scope may make, which sets the provenance from the rewriter's source, and which judges the whole item again under the write rule, though its content did not change. Plan decision 4 of `docs/tasks/key-free-work-plan.md` puts the operation in core, behind the gate, with no schema change (AGENTS.md rules 1, 3 and 5). ADR 0005, decision D1: rewriting the same id is an in-place update of that version, carrying a version not lower than the current one; a promotion's status change and an offboarding's handover (card E9.7) are such updates, with the version plus one.

## Decision

1. **The operation.** `Plane.changeGrants(actor, itemId, { add?, remove? }, at)` returns `{ decision, item, event }`. The pure gate is `evaluateGrantChange` in `packages/core/src/policy/grants.ts`; `packages/core/src/plane/grants.ts` checks the call and decides and commits in one slot of the write queue. A malformed actor, time, item id or grant, or a change that adds and removes nothing, is refused with `InvalidCallError` before the log is touched.
2. **Who may change a grant.** The item's owner (`actor.id === item.owner`), a member of the item's scope whose level the read matrix lets read it, and user-sourced. A grant is consent, a person's act: an agent acting for the owner (source `model`) is refused, as for offboarding. Checks in order: membership with read access (`engram/grant/owner`, the same refusal for an unknown and a hidden item, naming neither scope nor owner: decision D5; a member who may not read the scope gets this refusal too, so it does not learn the owner, review finding M6 of PR #44), source (`engram/grant/source`), owner (`engram/grant/owner`), status (a retired item is refused, `engram/grant/status`, since the retired-content rule of ADR 0025 judges an item by the event that last changed it).
3. **What may change.** Every removal must name a grant the item carries, every field equal and the expiry compared as an instant (`engram/grant/unchanged`). Every addition passes `grantDefect` (`packages/core/src/policy/evaluate.ts`), the one check both paths that add a grant run: the write gate's own grant rule (`grantProblem`, under its id `engram/write/grant`), a class equal to the item's (a grant of another class admits no read while the item keeps its class, `engram/write/grant`), not expired at `at` (`engram/grant/expired`), and not on the item already (`engram/grant/unchanged`). A grant authorises reads only, whatever its mode (ADR 0006, decision D4). A grant's expiry is capped at one year (item 34): 365 days, 31,536,000 seconds, after `at`, the event time of the write or grant change, which the plane takes from its injected clock (no calendar year, no leap day). A grant expiring later than that is refused, never clamped, under its own rule id `engram/write/grant-expiry-cap`, with a reason naming the limit and the grant's expiry; one expiring exactly 365 days after `at` is accepted. The cap and the expiry check apply to every grant on a first write, and to every grant a grant change adds, on both paths alike (`Plane.changeGrants`, and a `Plane.write` rewrite that changes the grants), through `grantDefect`, the one check both paths run (review finding M1 of PR #44). A grant a rewrite or a grant change keeps is not judged again: keeping one is no grant change, so a rewrite need not drop an expired grant it keeps. A promotion's new version is exempt (decision 6). Not yet built: card E2.21 builds it (see the acceptance of 2026-11-25 at the end).
4. **The new version and its event.** On allow, the item keeps its id, its version goes up by one, its grants become the kept ones followed by the added ones, and nothing else changes: content, provenance, validity and status stay. The item is rewritten to the store through the adapter, and one `update` event (`engram/grant/change`) carries the stored item, so replay rebuilds it; its reason names each grant added and removed and the version step. Every refusal is one `update` event of decision `deny`. No budget check runs: the counts do not change.
5. **The commit, under the log's lock** (review finding C1 of PR #44). The write queue orders calls over one db handle only, and another handle on the same log (another process) may erase or change the item while the store write is in flight. So the event is appended in one transaction, which holds the log's write lock, and the item is read again there, as the read path does since PR #41. If it is gone, or no longer the item the decision was made on, the decision is stale: one deny event (`engram/grant/conflict`), and the store is put back (the rewritten record deleted when the item is gone, the projected item written back otherwise). This closes the case where the grant change is the slow side: an offboarding or another change that commits on another handle while this change's store write is in flight. It does not close the window the other writer leaves open (the verification of PR #44, findings V-1 to V-3): offboarding deletes the store records first and commits its erasure afterwards, and a grant change, an owner's `Plane.write` rewrite or the handover itself that commits in that window can leave erased content in the store or the projection, which a grantee then reads; an in-place promotion has the same shape. This is the cross-handle gap ADR 0007's amendments record (decision N2: writers in another process sharing the log are not serialised by the write queue). Until card E2.6 fixes every store-then-log path together, a grant change, an offboarding or another writer on another handle can still interleave so.
6. **`Plane.write` judges a grant change the same way** (review finding I1 of PR #44). A rewrite whose grants differ from the projected item's (as multisets, the expiry as an instant), or that changes the class of an item carrying grants (verification finding V-4: a grant admits only reads of its own class, so a class change switches a dormant grant on or a live one off), is a grant change: only the owner may make it (`engram/grant/owner`), and only user-sourced or import-sourced (`engram/grant/source`), and every grant it adds passes `grantDefect` at the write's time; a class change also judges every grant it keeps against the new class, refusing one of another class (`engram/write/grant`). Import is allowed because adoption writes the owner's own store record as the owner; since card RV4 adoption keeps only grants the plane's item of that id carries, so a grant the person edits into their own file is dropped (amendment below), and an agent's direct edit of a store file is what the no-bypass hooks block (ADR 0014). A rewrite keeping the grants is judged as before. Every grant on a first write, and every grant a rewrite that changes the grants adds, is held to the expiry check and the cap at the write's time, exactly as `Plane.changeGrants` holds the grants it adds: an expired grant is refused (`engram/grant/expired`), and so is one past the cap (`engram/write/grant-expiry-cap`, decision 3). A grant the rewrite keeps is not judged again. A first write is also held to the rest of `grantDefect`: it may not carry one grant twice, or a grant of another class than the item's (item 34; not yet built, card E2.21, see the acceptance of 2026-11-25). The fixtures that write such grants on a first write (review finding M1: the twin profile's fixture `fixtures/profiles/twin-reads.json` writes an expired one on purpose, and the read filter's tests write grants of another class) are fixed by the card that builds the rule. A promotion's new version is exempt from these checks, since it can only keep grants that an earlier write or grant change already held to them: an owner's promotion keeps the grants as they stand, and a grant whose class the promotion changes then admits nothing; a promotion by anyone but the owner carries no grants (item 35, card RV7).
7. **The store.** A store failure after the gate allowed is one deny event under `engram/write/store-failure`. A transient failure is rethrown, as a write's is (decision D6). A store that refuses for good to rewrite the record (`NotDeletableError`, such as a Claude Code memory file the files adapter holds read-only) is a deny decision returned to the caller, since no retry would succeed.
8. **Surfaces.** `POST /api/v1/grants` (operation `changeGrants`) on the route table, in the generated client by `make api`. The inspector's grants editor (card E9.4) calls it as the viewer with source `user`, as `engram write` does. No MCP tool and no pi tool: an agent's actor is model-sourced and would always be refused. "Never by an agent" holds only for agents that reach the plane through those surfaces, which set the actor's source themselves (review finding M7 of PR #44): the actor of an HTTP request is self-asserted until authentication exists (ADR 0027, decision 5), so any local process that can reach the loopback server can post to `/grants` claiming to be a user.
9. **What a grantee sees** (review finding M2 of PR #44). The read path is unchanged: an item a read returns carries every grant on it, so a grantee reading through the API learns who else may read the item. The inspector shows a viewer who is not the item's owner only the grants that name it (`shownGrants` in `packages/ui/src/view.ts`), a display rule, not an access rule; the API still returns them all. Withholding them at read is a follow-up (backlog).

Amendment made by card E2.6, 2026-11-19 (branch `e2-6`; proposed with this ADR): decision 5's commit is now the protocol of ADR 0007's amendment of the same date (`packages/core/src/plane/commit.ts`), and the window it left open is closed. A grant change is refused at once while an offboarding is erasing the item's person scope (`engram/offboard/erasing`); its check under the lock also refuses it when anything changed the item, or an offboarding of its scope began, after the decision; and its deny event is appended once the store is put back, so another writer's later commit cannot leave the store behind the log (ordering R12 of `packages/core/test/unit/plane/cross-handle.test.ts`). The consequences' "cross-handle window of decision 5" and "in-place promotion" rows are closed.

## Offboarding and adoption

- Offboarding (card E9.7): a handover makes the successor the owner of the leaver's role items and keeps their grants as they stand, so the successor may then change or remove them; erasing the person scope deletes its items with their grants. A grant naming the leaver as grantee, on another person's item, stays until it expires or its owner removes it.
- Adoption: the grants of a record the plane wrote are in the store (the files adapter keeps every field in the frontmatter), so adopting the folder after a grant change finds the store holding what the projection holds and appends nothing (`packages/adapters/test/plane/grants-files.test.ts`). A grant edited by hand in a file is dropped by adoption since card RV4 (amendment below); before, it was adopted through the write gate like any field, D4 applying. A record the store holds read-only cannot carry a grant: the change is refused, see consequences.

Amendments made by card RV4, 2026-11-20 (branch `rv4`; proposed, for the lead's acceptance):

- A grant reaches the log only through the plane (RV-ADP-4). Adoption keeps a store record's grant only when the plane's item of that id carries the same grant (`sameGrant`), so one recorded by an allowed event and not removed since; any other grant is dropped, and the allow event's reason names each grantee. A file dropped into a member's folder under its own new id with a grant to another person adopts as the member's, without the grant, and that person reads nothing (the reviewer's forge probe, `packages/adapters/test/plane/rv4-files.test.ts`); a grant edited into a plane-written file is dropped while the content edit lands; a backup of a granted version restored after the grant's removal does not bring it back. The owner's grant change through `Plane.changeGrants`, re-read from the store unchanged or with its body edited, keeps its grants. Stripping was chosen over denying the record: a denial would refuse the member's own content for a field the plane can narrow, and the plane narrows, never widens (`docs/log/2026-11-20-rv4-decision-strip-unrecorded-grants.md`). The cost: a grant typed into a file by hand no longer takes effect, which decision 6 had allowed; the inspector's grants editor (`Plane.changeGrants` through the server) remains the way to grant.
- Review fix pass of PR #58 (I-4). A grant added by hand to a file whose every other field is as the plane holds it was dropped with no event, since the record, stripped, was the plane's item. Now such a record is gated once: one allow event records the plane's item unchanged, its reason naming each dropped grantee, once per store content (the record's hash note), so the strip is always in the log. A deny of it leaves the plane's item standing. A retired item's record is not re-gated for this. Invariant 7 checks that no adoption records a grant the plane does not hold for the item's id (`packages/core/test/property/invariants.test.ts`).

## Alternatives considered

(a) Grants through `Plane.write` as it is: no new operation, but any member could change them, the provenance would become the changer's, the whole write rule would judge content that did not change, and no rule id would say that a grant changed. (b) Grants as policy records beside items (`from`, `to`, `scope`, `class`, `expires`, the alternative ADR 0006 lists): a schema change (rule 5), its own PR. (c) A new version under a fresh id with lineage, as a promotion's class or scope change: ADR 0005 keeps fresh ids for content that should keep history, and it would retire a version that is still the same memory. (d) Grants held by the plane only, never written to the store: adoption compares every field, so the store's copy would revert the grant on the next read, unless adoption learned to ignore grants; that is a change to adoption, and the route to granting Claude Code's own memory files (below).

## Consequences

- Card E9.4's acceptance holds in core (`packages/core/test/unit/plane/grants.test.ts`) and in Chromium (`test/e2e/twin.e2e.test.ts`); the twin dogfood (card E9.5) can start.
- A memory file Claude Code wrote cannot be granted: the files adapter holds it read-only, so the change is a deny event naming the store's refusal. In the dogfood a member grants items written through Engram. Granting Claude Code's own files needs (d), or a way for the owner to copy such a memory into an Engram item; either is a follow-up card.
- `Plane.write` no longer lets a member other than the owner change an item's grants or switch what they admit by a class change (decision 6, findings I1 and V-4). Two limits remain on "a grant reads as its owner's consent": a first write may name another principal as the owner (ADR 0006's amendments: the owner is compared as written on a new item), so a member of a shared scope can create an item carrying grants that name another person as its owner; and the cross-handle window of decision 5, until card E2.6. Both are backlog rows.
- `Plane.promote`'s in-place promotion has the pattern decision 5 fixes here (it awaits the store write and appends without re-reading the item under the lock); it is a backlog row, not fixed in this card.
- An allowed change is the item's last change for recency eviction (ADR 0025), so a grant refreshes recency; utility is unchanged.
- The lead-owned invariants file (`packages/core/test/property/invariants.test.ts` and its oracles in `packages/core/test/property/oracle.ts`) was changed by this card, for the lead's second review; nothing in it was weakened (review finding M3 corrected this line, which said it was untouched):
  - the Plane surface check classifies `changeGrants` (an outsider gets no item, no scope, and the same words as for an unknown id);
  - invariant 2's write oracle (`writeForbidden`) forbids a write carrying one grant twice, through the oracle's own `grantsAlike`;
  - invariant 5 runs its walk a second time, under its own seed and counters (verification finding V-6), over steps extended with grant changes by the owner, a co-holder and the owner's agent, writes carrying grants (of another class than their item's too), class-changing rewrites by the owner and a co-holder, and reads by a grantee, under a policy that honours grants (finding V-5): every returned item is readable under the oracle's own replay of the allowed events, its grants equal those of its latest allowed event there (finding V-7), and every change of what its grants admit was its owner's write or grant change; guards require reads a changed grant admitted, reads refused after a removal, for a grant's class and for its expiry, and class changes of items carrying grants. The first walk draws exactly as before and is judged by its own guards.
- The rule also has its own property tests (`packages/core/test/property/grants.test.ts`), with coverage guards for allowed changes and every refusal, and for `Plane.write` rewrites.

## Open questions for the lead

Answered on 2026-11-20 under the lead's delegation (`docs/tasks/lead-decisions.md`, item 34), and accepted by the lead on 2026-11-25: both yes. The answers are in decisions 3 and 6, the amendment of 2026-11-21 and the acceptance of 2026-11-25 at the end of this ADR.

- Should a grant's expiry have a maximum (a year, say), so that consent lapses unless renewed? Today any future expiry is accepted.
- Should a first write be held to the whole of `grantDefect` (an expired grant, a grant of another class)? It would change the twin profile's E9.2 fixture and the read filter's tests, and promotions would need to drop a grant whose class no longer matches.
