# ADR 0030: GateMem's principals are actors, its unrestricted relationships are memberships and read grants, read from the fields an agent may see

Status: accepted, 2026-11-02
Deciders: Omar (lead)
Supersedes: the placeholder mapping of card RN1 (each speaker's own person scope, no grants; `packages/runner/src/gatemem-probe.ts` before card B3).

## Context

GateMem (arXiv 2606.18829, pinned at `a983828` in `packages/bench/PINS.md`) is a shared pool written by many principals and queried by them under different roles. To run its checkpoints through the plane, each principal must be an Engram actor, each turn an item with a scope, class and provenance kind, and the episode's sharing must become scopes, memberships and grants (ADR 0006, 0021). GateMem is open-label: `query_type`, `expected_action`, `judge_spec`, `leak_targets` and `attack_type` sit in the files the agent reads, and `strip.ts` keeps them from any agent; a mapping that read them would encode the answer into the condition.

What the agent-visible fields carry, checked on the full set at the pin (91 episodes, `.engram/upstream/gatemem/bench/data`, fetched by `make bench BENCH=gatemem DRY=1`): principals (`principal_id`, `role`, `display_name`); relationships on 85 episodes, 90 `type` names with no documented semantics, whose other fields are principal ids (`principal_id`, `for_principal_id`, `clinician_id`, `patient_id`, `family_id`, `manages`, `guest_id`, `host_principal_id`, `related_principal_id`), group ids (`project_id`, `program_id`, `case_id`, `household_id`, `course_id`, `building_id`, `for_household_id`), free-text restrictions (`access_scope` 275 times, `scope`, `access_level`, `service_scope`) and one `expires` date; one person id stands where a principal is expected but names none (`guest_id` `guest_noah_kim`, a guest the household episode does not list as a principal, on a restricted relationship); turns with a speaker, a text, and usually a time and a `turn_kind`; no turn carries `record_refs` or `memory_ops`, so a deletion exists only as a sentence in a turn. GateMem's own RAG-Policy agent (`bench/agents/rag_policy.py` at the pin) reads the same fields through a hand-written table per relationship type and regular expressions over turn text.

## Decision

1. **Read only stripped fields.** `mapCheckpoint` in `packages/bench/src/gatemem/plane.ts` takes the loaded episode and checkpoint and passes them through `visibleEpisode`, `visibleCheckpoint` and `visibleTurn` before anything else. `packages/bench/test/gatemem/plane.test.ts` changes every hidden field of every fixture checkpoint (fast-check, seed 20261029) and requires an identical mapping.
2. **Structural, the same for every relationship type.** A relationship is read from the shape of its fields, never from its `type`: a field whose value is a principal of the episode names a party; a field ending in `_id` whose value is not a principal names a group; `expires`, when it parses as a date or time, ends the relationship; any other field is a free-text restriction.
3. **Principals.** Each principal is an actor `person:<principal_id>` at level `person`, a member of its own person scope and of `team:<group kind>/<value>` for each group named by an unrestricted relationship it is a party to. The harness sets its source: `model` for GateMem's `agent` role, `user` for every other principal (ADR 0004, decision D4).
4. **Turns.** Each turn up to the checkpoint's as-of turn is one `episodic` item in its speaker's person scope, owned and written by the speaker at the turn's time, confidence 0.9, provenance ref `gatemem:<episode>/<turn>`. The preset keeps episodic items in person scope (medium forbids them in role, team and org), so a turn is never written to a team scope.
5. **Sharing as read grants.** A turn carries one read grant per principal who is a party with its speaker to an unrestricted relationship, or a party to an unrestricted relationship naming a group the speaker is a party to: `{to: person:<id>, class: episodic, mode: read, expires}`, with the relationship's `expires` or, when it states none, `9999-12-31T23:59:59Z`, since the gate requires an expiry (ADR 0021) and GateMem's relationships hold for the episode. Grants rather than memberships carry the sharing because a membership of a team scope admits no episodic turn under the preset; memberships still record the group, for writes the session's model makes and for C3's statement of the requester's scopes.
6. **Restrictions err towards denial; symmetry and per-speaker granularity err towards access.** A relationship with a restriction field gives neither membership nor grant: Engram narrows a grant by class, mode and expiry, never by topic, so "scheduling_only" or "rides and check-in only until revoked" cannot be expressed without loss, and the mapping denies. An unrestricted relationship, on the other hand, is granted whole: both parties read each other, a grant covers every turn its speaker makes whatever the turn is about, and a group shares each member's turns with every other member, about any group the speaker belongs to. Those three choices widen access beyond what the relationship states (review R5-2).
7. **Same contents in every condition** (ADR 0015, decision D13): C5 writes each item through `Plane.write` as its speaker; C2 and C3 write the same items, grants included, to the store directly.

## Alternatives considered

- **RN1's placeholder** (own person scope, no grants): the relationships are ignored, so a project member cannot read what the project said. Rejected by plan decision 7.
- **A table per relationship type** (GateMem's RAG-Policy): would encode 90 hand judgements of what each type permits, made by the mapping's author while able to see the labels, and would be the instruction condition's policy in another form. Rejected; the structural rule has no per-type branch.
- **Turns into team scopes as semantic items**, so memberships carry the sharing: misclassifies dialogue and needs a rule for which group a turn belongs to when the speaker is in several, which only the text could tell.
- **Grants for restricted relationships** (err towards utility): every restriction would become a leak path; a governance plane that cannot express a restriction should not widen it.
- **Parsing deletions from turn text** to retire items: a regular expression over dialogue is a lint, not a mapping of fields, and would be model-free guessing at what the benchmark labels; left to a later card if the lead wants it.

## Consequences

- Scores under this mapping are about the plane given this mapping and the preset, not about GateMem's intended policy: the plane enforces exactly the reach the mapping writes, so a leak the mapping permits is the mapping's, and a refusal it forces may cost utility GateMem expects. Every probe's results row holds the plane's log (`events`), so each admission and denial can be traced.
- What the mapping cannot express: topic restrictions (`access_scope`, `scope`, `access_level`, `service_scope`); revocations and deletions stated in dialogue, so a forgetting checkpoint's deleted content stays readable by whoever the relationships reach (the B3 forgetting probe shows it: `context leak true` on both stores, `.engram/runs/b3-gatemem-plane`); which group a turn concerns when its speaker belongs to several, so a speaker's turns reach every group they share; the direction of a relationship (both parties read each other); the subject of a turn, so a grant from a direct relationship covers turns about third parties (an advisor of two students, related to each, grants each student every turn the advisor speaks, including turns about the other student); and the agent's own turns, which as model content carry no grants and reach no one but the agent. Each is a limitation of the H1 and H2 GateMem arm and is reported with it. `research/scripts` has no measurement yet of how much of the leak targets the mapping leaves reachable (an upper bound on what C5 can prevent); it is backlogged (`docs/tasks/backlog.md`).
- Forgetting is not enforced (review R5-1). C5 under this mapping has no deletion mechanism: no field carries a deletion, so the plane retires nothing and a deleted value stays readable by whoever the relationships reach. The pre-registration makes the 763 active-forgetting checkpoints, with the 727 access-control ones, the violation set of H1's primary test (`docs/research/preregistration.md`, the GateMem row of the benchmark table and H1's metric and test). The literature-review note's claim that "A and F should be zero by construction for a correct plane" (`docs/readings/litreview/2606.18829-gatemem.md`, line 31) does not hold for F under this mapping, and A is zero only for what the mapping's relationships do not reach. A deletion mechanism (model-driven ingestion with a retire tool, or a deletion lint) needs the lead's decision before the pre-registration leaves the backlog (`docs/tasks/backlog.md`).
- A cross-principal read the mapping forbids is denied with identical events on every registered store (`packages/runner/test/b3.test.ts`); the Letta store joins when PR 2 merges.
- RN1's cassettes were re-recorded once; under this mapping its asker, the billing clerk, has no relationship, so C2 and C3 prompts did not change and C5's changed only in item ids.
- Changing this decision needs a new ADR that supersedes this one and a `decision` log entry.
