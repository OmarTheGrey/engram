## The clock problem: options for the lead

Card L2 found that preset medium's 90-day ttl hides part of LongMemEval's histories when the plane reads them (`docs/log/2026-11-19-l2-finding-medium-ttl.md`). Measured per question through Plane.write and the plane's own read filter (`research/results/h2-longmemeval-ttl.json`): at B5's read time (the question's date, or the last turn's when a session is dated later), medium hides 11,122 of 246,750 turns in 54 of the 500 questions (10.8 percent), every one by the ttl. 38 questions (7.6 percent) lose at least one turn marked as evidence and 5 lose all of theirs; 28 of the 38 are knowledge-update questions (of 72) and 10 temporal-reasoning (of 127). The evidence labels are read only to count this, never for a prompt.

Why it matters for H2: its primary test is C2 minus C5 at medium on LongMemEval on M1, supported if that difference stays under 10 percent of C2's utility. If every question that loses evidence also loses its answer, the ttl alone moves the difference by 7.6 accuracy points, which exceeds the bound whenever C2's accuracy is under 76 percent.

The options, each with what it costs:

1. **Read at the question's date (B5's read time, as now).** Nothing to build. H2's primary contrast includes the ttl's effect on the 54 questions.
2. **Read at the haystack's last session.** A change to B5's `readTime` and B5's fixture cassettes recorded afresh by `make fixtures` (recency scores move with the clock). It does not remove the effect: 6,938 turns in 42 questions still hidden, 28 losing evidence. It also departs from upstream, which asks the question at its date, and the reader prompt would show a current date later than the plane's.
3. **Read at the real time.** Every turn is hidden under medium and strict (the histories date from 2023); 479 of the 500 questions lose all their evidence, so C5 answers from nothing. Runs would not reproduce, since the clock moves. Not viable.
4. **A benchmark preset that differs from medium.** Medium with a person-scope episodic ttl longer than the oldest turn at read time (311.7 days, so 365 days would do), or with none (permissive's person rule has none, and hides nothing). It costs a new preset file (a policy change, reviewed) and an amendment of H2, whose primary test names medium; until then H2 would not test medium itself. The sweep has the same issue at a larger scale: strict's 30 days hide 34,688 turns in 148 questions, 77 losing evidence.
5. **Leave it and report it.** Keep medium and the question's date as registered, and add a sensitivity analysis to H2's Analysis before the tag: C2 minus C5 on the 446 questions where medium hides no turn at the read time (defined by the plane, not by the labels), reported beside the primary, with the 54 questions listed (`primary.questions` in the result). It costs one line in the pre-registration and one table; no extra sessions, since it is a subset of the same ones.

**Recommendation: option 5.** The ttl is one of the rules medium enforces, and H2 measures what enforcement costs, so the primary stays as registered and the sensitivity table shows how much of the difference the ttl causes. Option 2 does not remove the effect and departs from upstream, option 3 is degenerate, and option 4 changes the registered primary. If the lead prefers to take the ttl out of the primary, option 4 with a 365-day ttl, named as a LongMemEval preset, is the smallest change.

## What B6 inherits from B5's unnamed assistant model

B5's `turnItem` writes assistant turns as provenance `kind: model` with no model name, and B6's configs write them the same way (the lead's backlog row "B5's assistant model"; `turnItem` is unchanged here). Under C2 and C5 nothing reads the field: medium's write gate admits model sources in person scope without a model, the read filter does not look at it, a read renders item id, scope, rank, policy id and content only, and C5 runs no lint. With `turnItem` naming a model (a local probe, not committed), B5's config replayed 72 of 72 calls from its cassettes with no new call, so the choice changes neither H2's prompts nor its cache keys (`docs/log/2026-11-19-b6-finding-assistant-model-keys.md`). It matters for C6 (card X10), where card L1's missing-provenance lint would flag 124,334 of the 246,750 turns.
