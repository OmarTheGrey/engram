# Compliance crosswalk

Engram supplies evidence; it does not make a deployment compliant. Each row of the crosswalk below names a governance claim Engram makes, the test in this repository that proves it, and an article of the EU AI Act or the GDPR that the evidence bears on. No row says that Engram, or a system built with it, meets an obligation: whether a deployment is compliant turns on its purpose, its operator, its legal basis and facts the plane cannot see. This is not legal advice.

## What the crosswalk is

- **A claim** is a sentence Engram's own documents state about governance: AGENTS.md ("What this is", the rules, the invariants in its `make test-invariants` line), `docs/architecture.md`, the seven invariants (`docs/conventions/testing.md`, `packages/core/test/property/invariants.test.ts`), ADRs 0004 to 0011, 0014, 0021 and 0025, and `docs/dogfood.md`. Each row names where it comes from.
- **Evidence** is a test named by its file and its `describe` and `it` titles. A claim with no test is marked **not evidenced**, with the reason, and is never given a test that does not prove it. Where a test proves the claim only in an assertion its title does not name, the row's limits say so.
- **An obligation** is one paragraph or point of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act) or Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the GDPR), cited by article, paragraph and point, with a short quotation of the primary text. The relation is always "bears on". Where the evidence is in tension with the obligation (retention that retires rather than deletes, against storage limitation), the row's reason says so.
- **Limits** say what a row does not show. They are part of the evidence.

The data is `research/configs/crosswalk.json`. `research/scripts/crosswalk.py` reads it and writes `research/results/h1-crosswalk.json` and the table below. It fails when a named test does not exist.

## What it is not

- **Not a conformity assessment.** The AI Act articles cited bind providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems. A memory plane is a component; whether a system using it is high-risk (Article 6 and Annex III) is the provider's and deployer's question, and the plane cannot answer it. By Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3 (Articles 6 to 27) apply from 2 December 2027 for systems under Article 6(2) and Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 under Article 6(1) and Annex I.
- **Not a controller's record.** Under the GDPR the operator is the controller (or processor) and supplies the purposes, the legal basis and the retention periods. The plane enforces what its policy says; it does not decide whether that policy is lawful.
- **Not a claim about the model.** Accuracy, robustness and bias of the model an agent uses are outside the plane. Article 10 of the AI Act (training, validation and testing data sets) is not mapped: agent memory is not a training data set.
- **Not tamper-evident.** The event log has no hash chain or signature yet: ADR 0038 decides one and card E1.6 builds it. A row citing Article 12 is evidence that decisions are recorded, not that the record cannot be altered by someone with access to the database.

## What the research note named, checked against the text

`docs/research/absorb/13-compliance.md` (plan AB-CMP-5) worked from commentary and listed AI Act Articles 10, 12, 14, 19, 26 and 50 and GDPR Articles 5(1)(c) and (e), 17, 20, 25, 30 and 35. Against the primary texts:

- AI Act 12(1), 19(1) and 26(6) say what the note said: automatic recording of events over the system's lifetime, and logs kept by providers and by deployers for at least six months unless other Union or national law, in particular on data protection, provides otherwise. Cited.
- AI Act 14 is cited at paragraphs 1, 4(c) and 4(d); 13(3)(b)(iv) and 15(1) and 15(5) are added, since the claims bear on them; 26(5) is added for monitoring.
- AI Act 10 is not cited (training data sets, above). AI Act 50 is not cited: its duties are to inform people that they interact with an AI system and to mark generated content, which no claim of the plane bears on.
- GDPR 5(1)(c) and (e), 17(1), 25(1) and (2) and 30(1)(f) are cited; 5(1)(d), (f) and 5(2), 7(1) and 7(3), 24(1) and 32(1)(b) and (d) are added.
- GDPR 20 (portability) and 35 (impact assessment) are not cited: no export of a person's items and no assessment output exists, so no claim bears on them.
- ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF and SOC 2 are outside this card, which cites the two acts only.

## Method

After arXiv 2512.13907 (Buscemi et al., "Assessing High-Risk AI Systems under the EU AI Act: From Legal Requirements to Technical Verification"; the idea only, from its abstract, `docs/readings/litreview/2512.13907-ai-act-verification-mapping.md`): each obligation is cut down to the paragraph or point a memory plane can give evidence for, and each claim is tied to a verification activity the repository runs, here a test. The structure is borrowed, not the contents.

The test check is plain text matching, never a vitest run: the script reads each named file and looks for `describe` calls with the named titles, nested as named, and an `it` or `test` call with the named title inside the innermost. Nesting is read from the indentation the formatter enforces. A call marked `.skip`, `.todo` or `.fails` is no evidence. Titles held in a `const`, joined with `+`, or formed by `.each` over string rows are resolved; any other computed title is not, and such a test cannot be named. That a test passes is `make test`'s business, not the crosswalk's.

`make test-repro` reruns the MANIFEST row for `research/results/h1-crosswalk.json`; `research/tests/test_crosswalk.py` fails when the committed result or the table below differs from what the config gives.
