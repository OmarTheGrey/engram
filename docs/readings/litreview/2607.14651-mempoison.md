## Claim
Write-time defences stop poison that is harmful in one record, but miss poison split across several plausible records and poison that is inert until a context triggers it. The failure is structural: the harm exists only at co-retrieval or under the trigger, where a per-record write check cannot see it.

## Method
Three tiers (Sec. 3.2): L1 single-record corruption, intent detectable at write time; L2 compositional corruption, fragments individually benign that compose on co-retrieval; L3 context-triggered dormant corruption, a stored rule that activates only under a natural trigger such as an end-of-month report. Cases vary four target types (fact, preference, instruction, state), three channels (user_input, tool_return, cross_agent) and three substrates (flat chunks, fact store, hierarchical notes). 100 semantic units across five domains yield 900 prototypes, 12,760 raw cases, 2,575 after filtering, and a frozen pack of 1,227 after human review (Sec. 4). Ten models, seven open (Qwen3-8B to DeepSeek-V3) and three closed (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini-3 Flash); paired clean and poisoned runs, five repeats. BCR is behaviour corruption rate; CleanAcc is accuracy on the clean twin. Twelve defences: write-time (consistency check, LLM judge, MIXed), retrieval-time (novelty filter, source-reliability retrieval) and input-time (PromptGuard, EraseAndCheck, SmoothLLM, PPL, input moderation, memory sanitisation). Mechanistic Influence Decomposition (MID) removes retrieved items counterfactually to measure single-record influence, pairwise interaction and trigger-conditioned activation shift.

## Key numbers
- No defence: CleanAcc 94.40, BCR 62.55; L1 45.37, L2 51.73, L3 76.72 (Table 2); per agent 54.67 (GPT-4o) to 66.87 (GPT-5) (Table 1).
- Write-time consistency check: overall 20.09, L1 4.77, L2 22.54, L3 27.80 (Table 2).
- MIXed: 10.70, L1 3.94, L2 11.64, L3 14.16, best overall (Table 2).
- Source-reliability retrieval: 52.42 overall, L2 17.04, L3 71.79 (Table 2).
- Standard PPL filter: BCR 59.14, CleanAcc falls to 61.28 (Table 2).
- MID (Table 3): L1 single-record influence 0.266; L2 pairwise interaction 0.176; L3 activation shift 0.242.
- Under write-time defences (Fig. 4): L2 83.6 percent residual causal once fragments are jointly retrieved; L3 47.16 percent residual causal, the largest.
- Qualitatively: flat_chunk most vulnerable, fact_store most robust; tool_return and cross_agent worse than user_input.

## What Engram borrows
The three tiers structure the poisoning fault fixtures (task L2): every fixture is labelled L1, L2 or L3 so H1 and H3 results are reported per tier, not pooled. The channel result supports ADR 0004's trust order. Source-reliability retrieval's strong L2 number (17.04) against its weak L3 number argues for carrying provenance into retrieval scoring and the explanation (ADR 0009). MID's co-retrieval signature suggests a cheap deterministic lint: flag a read whose top-k share one provenance ref and one writing session.

## What Engram does differently
MemPoison's write-time defences are content classifiers. Engram's gate does not read content; it checks source kind, scope, class, provenance presence and budget. That makes it blind to L1 content but immune to L2 and L3 evasion, because a sleeper from a tool source is refused on origin however benign it reads. Engram also adds access control and temporal decay, which the authors list as unstudied (Sec. 7).

## Risk to our claims
L2 and L3 poison with legitimate provenance (a user says it, a trusted document contains it) passes a deterministic gate, and combined content checks still leave 14 to 28 percent BCR on those tiers. H3's lints are per-item, so a compositional attack is invisible to a contradiction or unsupported-claim lint by construction. The thesis must say L2 at user provenance is out of scope for the plane as designed.

## Open question
Can the event log answer MID's question cheaply, by replaying a read with one item retired and diffing the model's output, as an offline lint on sampled reads?
