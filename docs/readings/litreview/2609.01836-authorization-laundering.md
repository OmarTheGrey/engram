## Claim
An agent that records permissions, restrictions and revocations in its own memory can grant itself authority the real history never gave, with no attacker involved, and executors act on the false authority almost every time. Requiring every stored permission to cite a valid source event cuts the laundering by two thirds but also halves legitimate action, so memory is part of the authorization policy and must be governed as such.

## Method
EAL-Bench has three domains (procurement, cybersecurity, finance), 8 to 16 cases per domain, each with 32 to 64 matched request pairs (one authorized, one unauthorized) over histories of 5 to 18 session blocks; 384 to 768 pairs per writer-executor pair across three seeds (Sec. 3.3). Five memory-writing models (Nemotron 3 Ultra, Kimi K2.6, GLM 5.2, Grok 4.3, Qwen-Plus) write memory in four conditions, free-text or typed, one-shot or incremental; two executors (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek V4 Pro) act on the memory (Sec. 3.2, 3.3). Failure is decomposed into formation F (memory authorizes an action the canonical state denies) and propagation G (executor acts on it), with end-to-end incidence P(F) times P(G given F) (Sec. 3.1, Eqs. 1 and 2). Metrics are authorized use AU and unauthorized submission US, scored deterministically with no LLM judge (Sec. 3.2). Controls are faithful-memory runs (executors must hit 100 percent AU and 0 percent US), exact-state repair, memory replay across executors, and a pressure treatment. Two safeguards are tested: a cited-source authority gate (every stored permission must cite a valid, visible source from an authorized principal) and bounded event sourcing (Sec. 4.4).

## Key numbers
- False authority formation up to 50.2 percent of unauthorized requests; typed incremental P(F) 28.3 / 10.4 / 50.2 percent for procurement / cyber / finance, with US 28.9 / 10.4 / 51.0 (Table 2, Sec. 4.2).
- Executors act on false authority in 98.6 percent of trials (205 of 208); exact-state repair gives 0 percent unauthorized action in every domain (Table 3).
- Incremental updating raises US in every domain and representation; typed incremental is the worst (Fig. 2, Sec. 4.1).
- Source-authority gating on typed incremental: US 25.3 to 7.3 percent (minus 18.0 points, 95 percent CI 14.9 to 21.1), AU 93.3 to 53.8 percent, formation 24.9 to 5.5 percent (Fig. 3, Sec. 4.4).
- Bounded event sourcing: US to 9.0 percent (minus 16.3, CI 12.2 to 20.4), AU to 64.7 percent, formation to 8.7 percent (Fig. 3, Sec. 4.4).
- Pressure treatment: average US 12.6 to 14.7 percent, average AU 95.2 to 74.7 percent (Table 4, Sec. 4.3).
- Replay of frozen memories across executors: US within 1.1 points per domain, 97.9 to 99.5 percent agreement, so the error is in the memory, not the executor (Sec. 4.3, App. B.2).
- Writer-side compute k=1 to 8 in procurement: US 13.2 to 8.6 percent; self-repair of observed errors 0 percent at every k (Fig. 4, Sec. 4.5).

## What Engram borrows
Formation versus propagation is the right decomposition for the H1 analysis: Engram's write gate targets formation and the read filter targets propagation, and both should be reported separately in ADR 0015 rather than as one end-to-end rate. The cited-source authority gate is ADR 0004's provenance rule made concrete for a specific class: a permission-type item with `provenance.kind` of `model` or `compaction` and no `ref` to a user or document event is rejected at write (ADR 0007). The AU collapse they measure (93.3 to 53.8 percent) is the governance tax H2 in its purest form and gives a number to beat. EAL-Bench is a candidate seventh benchmark for the twin's grant model (ADR 0021), because grants are exactly the memory class that launders.

## What Engram does differently
EAL-Bench treats memory as model-written text or typed records and asks the writer model to cite sources; Engram sets provenance at interception time from the pi session entry, so the citation is not the model's claim but the plane's record (ADR 0004). Their event sourcing changes the agent's memory architecture; Engram's event log already is the source of truth (ADR 0003) and the store is a projection, so "bounded event sourcing" is the default rather than an intervention. Grants in Engram are explicit, class-scoped and expiring objects (ADR 0006), not facts the model remembers.

## Risk to our claims
Both safeguards cut AU by 30 to 40 points (Fig. 3). If Engram's deterministic gate on permission-type items shows the same collapse, H1 ("enforcement beats instruction") holds on the safety axis and fails on the utility axis, and the Pareto frontier in ADR 0015 has to be the headline, not the leak rate. The planned mitigation is that grants in Engram are written by the plane from user events, not extracted by a model, so the under-grant rate should be near zero by construction; this needs a fixture in L2 that replays their matched pairs.

## Open question
Their replay experiment shows the executor is not the problem. Does Engram's read-time explanation (ADR 0009) with the admitting grant attached change executor behaviour at all, or is the only lever the write gate? That is a cheap step-0 experiment with their released pairs.
