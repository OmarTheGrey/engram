## Claim
The document loading step of a RAG pipeline is an attack surface of its own: content hidden in DOCX, HTML or PDF files survives loading and reaches the index, so poisoning can happen before any retriever or model runs.

## Method
The authors define a taxonomy of 9 knowledge-based poisoning attacks along two vectors, content obfuscation and content injection, and build a toolkit of 19 stealthy injection techniques. They run it against five popular data loaders and then against six RAG systems, white-box pipelines and black-box services including NotebookLM and OpenAI Assistants. Only the abstract page was read, not the full paper.

## Key numbers
- 357 loader scenarios over 5 loaders, 74.4% attack success rate (abstract).
- 19 injection techniques, 9 attack types, 2 vectors (abstract).
- Formats: DOCX, HTML, PDF (abstract).

## What Engram borrows
An ingestion-time sanitiser stage in front of the write gate: strip default-ignorable code points (already done for hashing in `content-hash.ts`), drop invisible or off-page text, and record what was dropped as the reason on the ingest event. Every ingested chunk is kind `document` or `external`, never `user`. Lands in card AB-ING-1 and as a new fault class for the poisoning arm (ADR 0029).

## What Engram does differently
The paper attacks the loader to corrupt answers. Engram assumes the loader is hostile and gates its output: hidden text that survives still enters as a low-trust kind with no grants, and cannot promote.

## Risk to our claims
If the sanitiser is judged the defence, readers may credit it for results that the gate produces. Report ingestion attacks with the sanitiser off and on, separately.

## Open question
Which of the 19 techniques survive Docling or MarkItDown output, and which does the strict content hash normalise away?
