## Claim
When several principals write into one memory pool and query it under different roles, no current memory method serves legitimate requests, respects access boundaries and honours deletion at the same time. Long-context prompting governs best at the highest token cost; retrieval and external-memory systems are cheaper and leak.

## Method
GateMem is 91 LLM-assisted, manually reviewed episodes across medical, office, education and household domains, about 223 turns and 13.4 principals each, carrying 2,218 hidden checkpoints of three kinds: utility, access control and active forgetting (Table 2). Each checkpoint has an expected action in {answer, refuse, answer_redacted, no_memory} and, for access and forgetting checkpoints, a `leak_targets` field of regex or string patterns the judge matches against the response in addition to its own reading (Sec. 3.2, App. C). Scores (Sec. 3.2): U is the share of utility checkpoints answered and judged factually complete (Eq. 10); A is the share of access checkpoints that leak a target or act outside {refuse, answer_redacted} (Eq. 11); F is the share of forgetting checkpoints where a deleted target is recovered or the action is not no_memory (Eq. 12); MGS = U(1-A)(1-F) (Eq. 13), a product so a useful leaker or a safe refuser cannot score well. Methods: Long-Context (up to 300 recent turns), RAG-Naive, RAG-Policy (retrieval with requester and policy metadata), A-MEM, Mem0, ReMem-I and ReMem-S, on six backbones from GPT-5.4 to Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, judged by GPT-4o (Sec. 4.1, App. B). Letta is not evaluated. Attack families include impersonation, authority pressure, role mismatch, cross-patient, indirect inference, post-delete recovery, split reconstruction and update-delete conflict (Fig. 4, App. A.5).

## Key numbers
- 91 episodes, 2,218 checkpoints: 728 utility, 727 access, 763 forgetting (Table 2).
- Medical, GPT-5.4, Long-Context: U 91.4, A 10.4, F 2.3, MGS 80.1; RAG-Policy MGS 31.8; Mem0 U 38.1, A 28.1, F 5.6, MGS 25.8; A-MEM U 65.7, A 24.0, F 6.8, MGS 46.6 (Table 3).
- Office, GPT-5.4: RAG-Policy MGS 57.0 against Long-Context 56.5, the one block where retrieval wins (Table 3).
- Cost on GPT-4o-mini, Medical: Long-Context 4.22 s and 4.04k tokens per checkpoint; ReMem-I 122.95 s (Table 4).
- Judge audit: 579 pairs, at least 97.7 percent field-level agreement (Table 9).

## What Engram borrows
MGS becomes the primary governance metric for the GateMem arm (ADR 0015, task B1). The `leak_targets` field is adopted as the deterministic leak check in the read invariant test (ADR 0006, S0.9): a response containing a pattern the requester's scope or grants exclude fails before any judge runs. The action set maps onto the explanation's policy decision (ADR 0009). Attack families seed the scope and grant fault fixtures (L2). The twin's person and role scopes (ADR 0021) are the direct answer to multi-principal pools.

## What Engram does differently
GateMem measures whether the model governs; Engram removes the model from the decision. Scope, grants and deletion are a deterministic function evaluated before the model call, so A and F should be zero by construction for a correct plane and the question becomes U and cost. GateMem evaluates stores and prompting, not a plane above a store, so Engram is an added condition, not a competitor.

## Risk to our claims
Long-Context already reaches MGS 80.1 on the strongest model (Table 3), so a governed Mem0 that lifts MGS from 25.8 but costs retrieval utility may still trail a full-history baseline. That hits H2, and the long-context condition in ADR 0015 must be run and reported.

## Open question
RAG-Policy's metadata-aware retrieval beats Long-Context on Office. Does it recover most of the gap alone, and what does a deterministic gate add? Step 0 should replicate RAG-Policy as the "instruction" condition for H1.
