## Claim
Fusion of lexical and dense retrieval for agent memory should be a per-query decision. A cascade that reads the BM25 top-k margin and only then runs the costly dense channel keeps accuracy while skipping most dense calls.

## Method
A retrieval substrate that picks among BM25, dense, RRF and an agent-aware RRF, and decides per query whether the dense channel (about 52 ms) is worth running, from the BM25 margin alone. Evaluated on LongMemEval (n=500) and LoCoMo (n=1,982) with LLM judges, and on 9 BEIR datasets against Pyserini and PISA. Only the abstract was read.

## Key numbers
- LongMemEval: the cascade skips 63% of queries at parity LLM-judged accuracy, 2.67x faster (paired bootstrap p>=0.88) (abstract).
- LoCoMo: BM25 alone is strongest; the trigger skips 100% of dense calls, +0.089 Hit@5 (abstract).
- Gaps between BM25, dense and RRF accuracy were about 0.01 to 0.02 in a search snippet; not verified in the full text.

## What Engram borrows
The idea that the dense channel is optional and gated by a cheap lexical signal: a hybrid tier in core that runs only when the caller supplies vectors, and reports which channel contributed. Lands in AB-RET-2.

## What Engram does differently
Engram needs every score component reportable, and every item admitted by the read filter before it is ranked. A learned router is harder to explain; we would use a fixed rule, logged with the policy id.

## Risk to our claims
BM25 already saturates on LongMemEval-style corpora, so a vector tier may show no gain on H2 and must not be claimed as one.

## Open question
Does a BM25 margin gate stay stable on Engram's small per-scope item sets?
