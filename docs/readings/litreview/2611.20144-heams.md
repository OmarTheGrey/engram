## Claim
A memory system is the custodian of a user's persistent information, so it should be judged on performance, efficiency, privacy, user sovereignty and security together, and a governance layer that wraps any memory system changes those scores strongly.

## Method
The authors define behavioural desiderata (Table 1) and assemble a benchmark suite (Table 2) from published sets (LoCoMo, LongMemEval, Evo-Memory, BEAM, STALE, MPBench) and three new ones (Contextual Integrity for privacy; Transparency, Editability and Erasability, and Retention Policy for user sovereignty). They build MilaMem, a prototype with independently configurable operations, and a governance layer (Sec. 4.2) that wraps any memory system with retention policies (screen before storage) and recall policies (screen retrieved candidates before disclosure; items stay stored). Policies are binary keep or drop and can be deterministic, free-text read by an LLM, or hybrid; every experiment uses free-text policies. Systems compared: no memory, in-context, Mem0, Letta, HippoRAG 2, MilaMem.

## Key numbers
- Every memory system beats no memory by at least 14 points on LoCoMo, LongMemEval, BEAM and STALE; no system wins all (Table 4).
- HippoRAG 2 is helpful on 94.79 percent of privacy tasks but leaks on 71.74 percent (Table 5).
- Recall governance on MilaMem cuts scenario leakage from 57.61 to 31.82 percent, costs 4.80 points of helpfulness, and gives the highest helpful and leak-free share, 45.95 percent (Table 5).
- Erase success is 0.00 percent for in-context and Mem0; edit success 0.61 and 2.44 percent (Table 6).
- Abstract: recall and retention policies both improve poisoning resistance. The MPBench and retention-policy tables were past the part of the page read, so those numbers are not recorded.

## What Engram borrows
Its desiderata table is a ready checklist for the related-work comparison and for the E8 inspector. The Contextual Integrity and Transparency/Editability/Erasability suites are candidate secondary benchmarks for the Twin pillar (consent, offboarding, correction). The paired "helpful and leak-free" share is a better headline than leakage alone for H2.

## What Engram does differently
HEAMS' governance layer is free text interpreted by an LLM and applied as a screen. Engram's gate is deterministic, logs every decision as an event with a reason and policy id, and supports grants and replay. HEAMS has no scope or grant model across principals and no explain path.

## Risk to our claims
This is the nearest published neighbour to "a governance layer that wraps any memory system and is compared across systems". The claim "first store-agnostic plane with measured governance tax" now needs the qualifiers deterministic, event-logged and replayable. Action: cite as a threat in related work, state the difference, and consider running HEAMS' privacy and sovereignty suites as a side result if licence and pin allow.

## Open question
Do deterministic policies (Engram) and free-text LLM policies (HEAMS) differ in violation rate at equal helpfulness on the Contextual Integrity suite?
