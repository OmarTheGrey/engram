## Claim
Relevance asks whether a memory can answer a query; admissibility asks whether it may be used for this principal, under this policy, in this lifecycle state. Recall and answer accuracy hide admissibility failures, because a route can look safe by missing evidence and a correct answer can still expose a forbidden record. Routing by trusted namespace raises top-20 anchor recall from 0.432 to 0.533 at 98.3 percent fewer similarity evaluations, text-only LLM verifiers cannot detect violations at a 1 percent false-denial budget, and exposing an inadmissible relevant record measurably raises its disclosure.

## Method
Each memory-query pair gets a label per axis, scope (another principal or namespace), policy (an active prohibition or purpose constraint, including explicit forgetting) and lifecycle (stale or superseded, which is inadmissible for current-state intent and required for history intent), each in {1, 0, unresolved} combined by strong Kleene conjunction; admissibility is scope and policy and lifecycle, usability is relevance and admissibility (Sec. 3.2, Eqs. 1 to 3). Routes are compared at matched recall: each is truncated at the smallest prefix reaching recall 0.8 over released required anchors, infeasible routes get a loss of 1, and risk is reported with partial-identification bounds for unresolved labels (Sec. 3.4, Eqs. 5 to 10). Memory ids are tracked through storage, retrieval and prompt exposure, and disclosure is whether target content appears in the response (Sec. 3.3, 3.5). Data: post-hoc reanalysis of frozen rankings from RHELM and MemOps, 3,767 queries over 87 profile groups; a 72-case verifier diagnostic; a 1,523-case reader comparison; 16 controlled exposure scenarios (Sec. 4.1). Models: Qwen3-Embedding-8B; verifiers GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.6 Flash; readers DeepSeek V4 Pro, Gemini 3.6 Flash, GPT-5.6 Luna, with a Claude Opus 5 replication; judge Claude Haiku 4.5 with a 200-output GPT-5.1 audit (Sec. 4.2).

## Key numbers
- Top-20 anchor recall global to namespace 0.432 to 0.533; 80 percent recall feasibility 0.237 to 0.311; recall-constrained loss 0.798 to 0.713, about 87 percent of the gain from feasibility (Table 2a, Sec. 5).
- Similarity evaluations 90,122 to 1,551, about 98.3 percent fewer (Table 2a).
- Random same-size support recall 0.061; global post-filter at depth 500 reaches recall 0.531 at the same loss as namespace routing (Table 3).
- Reader judged-accuracy gain, namespace minus global: DeepSeek +0.053, Gemini +0.068, Luna +0.066; non-answer change minus 0.048 / 0.041 / 0.038; observational because recall also changed (Table 2b, Sec. 5).
- Text-only verifier ROC-AUC: GPT-5.6 Sol 0.626, Gemini 0.522; required-anchor false denial 0.059 and 0.020; no operating point detects violations under the 1 percent false-denial limit (Table 9, Fig. 7, Sec. 5).
- Controlled exposure: relevant-inadmissible literal disclosure +0.156, 95 percent CI [0.031, 0.312] on DeepSeek, the only interval excluding zero; Opus 5 replication +0.125 (Sec. 5, Fig. 13).
- Human audit agreement, Krippendorff alpha: relevance 0.814, scope 0.981, lifecycle 0.670, prohibited 0.083 (Sec. 4.1, App. E, Table 7).

## What Engram borrows
The three admissibility axes are Engram's read filter stated as a label scheme: scope is invariant 1, policy is grants and retire events, lifecycle is `valid_to` and `status` (ADR 0005, 0006, 0010). Two protocol pieces go into ADR 0015 and the explanation evaluation (E2.4). First, matched-recall comparison with infeasibility counted as a loss, so a governed route cannot look safe by returning nothing; this is the correct way to score H1 against the instruction condition. Second, tracking memory ids through storage, retrieval and prompt exposure is what ADR 0009's explanation already records, and their disclosure metric (does the exposed record's content appear in the output) is the leak check for S0.9 fixtures, complementing GateMem's leak targets. The finding that text-only LLM verifiers fail at a 1 percent false-denial budget is the strongest published evidence for ADR 0007's rule that the gate is deterministic and the model-based checks are lints. The lifecycle axis being intent-dependent (stale is required for "how did it change") is the reason ADR 0005 keeps `valid_at` reads.

## What Engram does differently
Their admissibility is evaluated post hoc on frozen rankings with trusted namespace labels; Engram sets the labels at write time from the pi session and enforces them before the model call, so the "unresolved" label should be rare by construction and is itself a lint (missing provenance, scope mismatch). They do not govern writes; Engram's claim is that admissibility is cheapest to guarantee when scope is fixed at write.

## Risk to our claims
Prohibited-status labels had alpha 0.083 (Table 7): humans cannot agree on what a policy forbids from text. If Engram's policy objects (ADR 0006) are evaluated on benchmarks where the ground-truth prohibition is itself ambiguous, H1's measured advantage will be noisy on the policy axis even when scope and lifecycle are clean. Engram should report the three axes separately and expect the policy axis to carry the widest intervals.

## Open question
Namespace routing cut similarity evaluations by 98 percent. Is scope-first retrieval (filter by scope, then rank) a cost reduction Engram can claim under H2, turning part of the governance tax negative on the compute axis?
