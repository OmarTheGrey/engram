## Claim
Store raw evidence first and immutably, derive beliefs only after deterministic validation, keep SQLite authoritative with vector and graph as rebuildable projections, and serve reads with zero LLM calls. This reaches 90.19 percent on LoCoMo with the memory held fixed while the answer model is swapped.

## Method
Tier 0 stores every user turn as an evidence row and extracts hard anchors (dates, money, versions, URLs) with regex and spaCy, no LLM (Section 4.1). Tier 1 has an LLM propose candidate facts that must pass four checks: lexical support in the source window, exact match of hard anchors, subject present in source, negation preserved (Eq. 1). Accepted facts link to their evidence row. Updates aim at one active fact per user, entity and fact type; superseded facts are kept (4.5). Retrieval is a rule table over query shape feeding vector, BM25, temporal and graph routes, fused with weighted RRF at k = 20 (4.5). `ReadResult` returns context and answer instructions as separate fields (4.6). Evaluated on LoCoMo C1 to C4 (1,540 questions, GPT-4o judge), LongMemEval-S (500 questions) and BEAM (700 questions), with Sonnet 4.6, GPT-4o, Kimi K2.5 and Qwen3 32B as writer and answerer (Section 5).

## What Engram borrows
- Evidence before belief is ADR 0003 and 0004 in one sentence: the event log and evidence hash are authoritative, store state is a projection. Eywa's rebuild of LanceDB and the graph from SQLite is the `rebuild()` test in S0.8.
- The four checks of Eq. 1 are deterministic and become gate rules or lints (ADR 0007, 0008): missing provenance (no evidence row), unsupported claim (no lexical support); hard-anchor mismatch is a new seeded-fault fixture for L2.
- The failure breakdown in Table 11 (not retrieved, lost before context, not extracted) is the attribution the explanation record must support (ADR 0009).
- Separating `context` from `answer_instructions` is the rule that policy text in `custom_message` is never mixed with recalled items.

## What Engram does differently
Eywa governs faithfulness of one user's facts; it has no scope, grants or multi-principal reads, and item 16 admits erasure across projections and logs is untested. Engram adds scope and grants, makes erasure an event with a report (ADR 0010), and applies the same provenance rules over stores it does not own. Eywa validates once at write; Engram also lints after, because sources change.

## Risk to our claims
Eywa's zero-LLM read path and deterministic validation give much of what H1 promises inside one store, with no plane. H1's enforcement-beats-instruction result must therefore be shown on stores that lack this (Mem0, Letta, files), or H4 carries the thesis. Item 7 notes no ablations and no significance tests; our H1 and H3 must have both.

## Open question
Eywa rejects about 8.3 percent of candidate facts at write time (4.1). What is Engram's expected gate rejection rate on benign traffic, and above what rate does the governance tax (H2) come from rejected true facts rather than from latency?
