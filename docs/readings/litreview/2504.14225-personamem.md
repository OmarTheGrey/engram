## Claim
Frontier models can recall what a user said and track a preference, but they fail about half the time to apply the user's current state to a new query, and reasoning models do no better.

## Method
PersonaMem has 20 personas and over 180 simulated user-LLM histories of 10, 20 or 60 sessions, scaled to about 32k, 128k and 1M tokens, each session 15 to 30 turns over 15 task topics such as therapy, food, travel and legal advice, about 6k in-situ query-response pairs in all, generated synthetically with GPT-4o (Sec. 3). Seven query types: recall shared facts, suggest new ideas, acknowledge the latest preference, track the full preference evolution, revisit the reasons behind an update, give preference-aligned recommendations, generalize to new scenarios. The model picks the best response in a multiple-choice setting; open-weight models are also scored by generative likelihood at 32k. Fifteen models including GPT-4.1, o4-mini, GPT-4.5, o1, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, DeepSeek-R1, Llama-4-Maverick, Claude-3.7-Sonnet. RAG and Mem0 are run as proof-of-concept memory baselines at 32k (App. A.2).

## Key numbers
- GPT-4.1, o4-mini, GPT-4.5, o1 and Gemini-2.0-Flash score around 50 percent overall; Llama-4-Maverick about 43 (abstract, Sec. 4).
- Recall and preference tracking sit around 60 to 70 percent; applying the latest situation to a response sits around 30 to 50 (Sec. 4).
- Hardest types: suggesting new ideas, preference-aligned recommendations, generalizing to new scenarios (Sec. 4).
- Error analysis of 100 GPT-4o failures: 48 lacked personalization, 24 missed a preference update, 14 were format errors, 12 were hallucinations (Sec. 4.3).
- RAG beat Mem0 in most categories; Mem0 took about 150 s per query against 0.61 s for RAG (App. A.2).
- Inter-rater agreement for the best-response label among three authors: AC1 = 0.56 (App. A).
- Per-context-length accuracy is in figures that the text read does not reproduce.

## What Engram borrows
The seven query types become the twin's evaluation categories in E9.4, with preference evolution and the reasons behind an update as the ones that test bi-temporal validity directly: an update supersedes with lineage and a reason, never overwrites (ADR 0005). The 24 percent "missed a preference update" error class is the target for the expired-validity lint (ADR 0008). The RAG-against-Mem0 latency gap is a reminder that a store's native extraction has a cost the plane must meter (ADR 0017).

## What Engram does differently
PersonaMem tests whether a model can personalize from a raw history in context. Engram's twin removes that burden from the model: the plane returns the latest valid preference with its validity window and the superseded versions on request, so the model is not asked to infer evolution from a 1M-token transcript. Engram also scopes the twin: a successor sees the role scope, never the therapy sessions.

## Risk to our claims
Only 48 of 100 failures are about personalization; the rest are updates, format and hallucination. A governed read that hands the model the right item still leaves the model to use it, and the ceiling of around 50 percent suggests H4 could show correct recall with no downstream gain. Mitigation: E9.4 scores recall (did the plane return the current preference) separately from use (did the response follow it), as MERIT already forces for actions.

## Open question
Data is synthetic and the label agreement is moderate (AC1 = 0.56). Is a 50-item label audit (ADR 0015) enough here, or should the twin arm use dogfood histories with the person as the judge of their own preference?
