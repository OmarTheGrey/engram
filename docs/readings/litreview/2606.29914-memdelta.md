## Claim
Memory-system gains are confounded with model, embedding and retrieval-pipeline changes; changing one component at a time can reverse a ranking.

## Method
A protocol that varies one component at a time on LongMemEval-S (500 questions) across three model families.

## Key numbers
- Verbatim RAG 47.2% vs full-context GPT-4o-mini 49.8%, p = 0.34 (abstract page).
- Embedding change alone: +6.2pp, n = 500, p = 0.004.
- Self-memory 42% vs basic retrieval 47%.
- Mem0 vs cloud RAG on 2 of 6 question types: 72.7% vs 73.9%, n = 88, at about 50x cost.

## What Engram borrows
Report each result with the other components held fixed, stratified by model, plus a write-path cost column, in H2's LongMemEval tables.

## What Engram does differently
Engram's C1 to C5 vary one policy component per condition; MemDelta also stratifies by model.

## Risk to our claims
Rankings flip across model families, so a single-model result may not generalise. Reporting model slots M1 to M3 apart addresses this.

## Open question
Should a per-model stratified table be required in the preregistration?
