## Claim
A self-evolving skill library that accumulates skills without an outcome signal drifts: retrieval degrades, irrelevant skills get injected, and pass rate can fall below the no-skill floor. Three trace-level diagnostics on an append-only evidence log see the drift before end-task scores do, and a minimal governance recipe (outcome-driven retirement, a hard cap on active skills, an authoring prior) turns a flat +0.002 into +0.328 pass@1 over 100 rounds.

## Method
Library drift is defined as expected pass@1 under the library falling below the no-skill baseline on a fixed task distribution (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 1), with sub-modes stagnation, bloat and erosion (Sec. 3.2). Diagnostics (Sec. 4): a per-skill contribution score, successes minus failures over trials where the skill was injected; a Critic verdict per failure capsule labelled helped, hurt, neutral or inapplicable; and router engagement, the share of eval tasks assigned a skill. The Ratchet recipe (Sec. 5): retire when n(s) at least 100 trials and contribution at most minus 0.10; hard cap of 50 active skills with lowest-contribution eviction and a non-divergence bound (Prop. 1); a meta-skill document constraining the Synthesizer's output. Other defaults: dedup thresholds 0.85, minimum cluster size 3, at most 2 new skills per round, retrieval shortlist K=10, rollback threshold 0.10 held 5 rounds (Table 2, App. B). Setup: MBPP+ hard-100 (100 tasks sampled from the 378-task test split after discarding about 273 solved on all 5 baseline seeds; 60 train, 40 eval), 100 rounds, 3 seeds, Claude Opus 4.7 for every LLM call and Cohere embed-v4, with a single direct-call solver and no execution feedback (Sec. 6.1). Eight ablations A1 to A8.

## Key numbers
- Default: baseline 0.258 plus or minus 0.047, late-window mean 0.584, peak 0.658, gain +0.328 plus or minus 0.018; router 73 percent; 50 active / 89 retired (Table 1, Sec. 6.2).
- A1 no injection +0.002 plus or minus 0.005 (the floor); A4 harsh retirement minus 0.019 plus or minus 0.010 with 2 active / 51 retired (Table 1, Sec. 3.3, 6.3).
- A2 retrieval-only +0.077; A3 no meta-skill +0.187 (removal costs 43 percent of the Default gain); A5 no canon +0.374; A6 no guard +0.363; A7 cap=100 +0.317 plus or minus 0.110 with per-seed gains from +0.172 to +0.440; A8 meta refresh +0.372 at 55 percent more wall time (Table 1, Table 3, Fig. 2, Sec. 6.3, Sec. 7).
- Motivating SkillsBench figure: LLM-authored skills +0.0pp, human-curated +16.2pp (abstract; not re-run here).
- Cost: about 14.5k LLM calls per 100 rounds (10k solver, 4.3k critic, 152 synthesis), 43 percent above A1; wall time 6.5 h against 2.3 h (Sec. 6.4).
- Hoeffding deviation about 0.20 at N_min=100 and about 0.44 at N_min=20 (Sec. 3.3, 5.1).

## What Engram borrows
Three things land in ADR 0011 and the D-series cards. First, the retirement rule needs a minimum trial count: their A4 shows retiring on few trials destroys the library, and the Hoeffding argument gives Engram a principled N_min for the smoothed success rate ADR 0011 names. Second, a hard cap on active procedures per scope is a retention budget (ADR 0010) for the procedural class; their cap=100 ablation shows the cap mostly buys variance reduction. Third, the per-skill contribution score and the Critic verdict are exactly the "evidence attached" events ADR 0011 inherits from SKILL.nb, and the fact that they move before pass@1 does is the argument for making the drift signal an event stream, not a periodic re-score. The Critic verdict is a model-based lint (ADR 0008 tier 2) on procedural items.

## What Engram does differently
Their library is authored by a Synthesizer from solver traces on one benchmark; Engram induces procedures from pi session trees with AWM (ADR 0012) and admits them only after replay (ADR 0011), so the "accumulation without quality signal" stage they describe is blocked at admission rather than corrected by retirement. Engram also attributes each injection through the read explanation (ADR 0009), so the contribution score is derivable from the event log without a separate capsule store.

## Risk to our claims
Governance of the library costs 43 percent more LLM calls and nearly three times the wall time of the ungoverned run (Sec. 6.4). H5 claims governed procedural memory is net positive; this paper says the positive is large but the cost is real, and H2's knee may sit at a different place for procedural items than for semantic ones. Engram must meter the critic and replay calls (ADR 0017) and put procedural governance on its own frontier chart.

## Open question
Their solver has no execution feedback, so skills are the only signal. In pi, the agent runs tests; does the drift signal still lead pass rate when the agent can self-correct, or does execution feedback mask drift until the library is large?
