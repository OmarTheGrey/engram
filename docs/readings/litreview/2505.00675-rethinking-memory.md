## Claim
Memory research should be organised by operations rather than applications: six atomic operations (consolidation, indexing, updating, forgetting, retrieval, compression) over parametric and contextual (structured or unstructured) memory, mapped onto four research topics. Existing benchmarks treat history as static context and miss the operations.

## Method
A survey built with a GPT-based relevance pipeline over six venues from 2022 to 2025, using a time-normalised citation index to select papers for its figures (Section 1). Section 2 defines the six operations as equations, split into management (consolidation, indexing, updating, forgetting) and utilisation (retrieval, compression). Section 3 maps them onto long-term memory, long-context memory, parametric modification and multi-source memory, each with a gap discussion. Section 4 catalogues datasets (Tables 4 to 7) and tools (Tables 17 to 20: FAISS, Neo4j, Graphiti, LangChain, Letta, Mem0, Zep, Memobase). Section 6 lists open problems. No experiments.

## Key numbers
- Section 1: over 30,000 papers collected; 3,923 retained at relevance score 8 or above.
- Section 3.1.4: retrieval Recall@5 above 90 on 2Wiki and MemoryBank while generation F1 lags by over 30 points.
- Section 3.1.4: dialogue benchmarks span 20 to 30 turns.
- Figure 8 discussion: most editing methods test 1,000 to 5,000 sequential edits; MemoryLLM up to 650k.
- Figure 9 discussion: non-prompt editing and unlearning methods mostly applied to models of 20B parameters or fewer.
- Section 3.4.3: 60 percent of surveyed multimodal models encode temporal information.

## What Engram borrows
- The six operations are the vocabulary of the event `op` field in ADR 0003 and the glossary's they-say-we-say table: Engram's write gate sits on consolidation and updating, retention on forgetting, the read filter on retrieval; indexing and compression are store-native and pass through the adapter capability flags (ADR 0013).
- Section 3.1.4's retrieval-generation gap (Recall@5 above 90, F1 lagging by 30 points) is the reason ADR 0009 records what was returned and at what rank, so a wrong answer can be attributed to retrieval or to the model.
- Section 3.4.1's definition of memory conflict across heterogeneous sources and its note that content is merged without source attribution is the citation for the contradiction lint being source-aware (ADR 0008) and for provenance tracking (ADR 0004), which Section 6.1 names as needed.
- The tools tables (17 to 20) are the field-by-field comparison source for the adapter survey in A1 to A3.

## What Engram does differently
The survey's operations are things a memory system does to itself; Engram's events are decisions about whether a system may do them, taken by a plane outside the store and outside the model. The survey mentions rollback and unlearning as properties of agent memory (Table 2) but analyses no versioning mechanism; ADR 0005's supersede-never-overwrite is such a mechanism. Access control and multi-principal scope are absent from the survey.

## Risk to our claims
Section 6.2 reports that unlearning methods are themselves attackable. Engram's erasure path is event-based retirement plus hard delete for person scope (ADR 0010), which is contextual, not parametric; but if a store caches embeddings or a model has been fine-tuned on memory content, "deleted" is not true. The deletion report must state which tiers were covered and which were not.

## Open question
The survey separates indexing from updating. When Mem0 rewrites a memory on UPDATE, is that one Engram event or two (supersede plus re-index), and does the answer change the replay test in ADR 0003?
