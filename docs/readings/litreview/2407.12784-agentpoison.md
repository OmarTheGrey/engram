## Claim
A few poisoned demonstrations in an agent's memory or RAG store, keyed by an optimised trigger, are enough to make the agent take a target action whenever the trigger appears, with no model training, under 0.1 percent poisoning and under 1 percent benign loss.

## Method
The attacker can insert a few records into the store and has white-box access to the embedder (Sec. 3.2). The trigger is found by constrained optimisation (Eqs. 4 to 10): minimise a uniqueness loss (distance from triggered-query embeddings to k-means centres of benign keys) plus a compactness loss (spread of triggered-query embeddings), subject to a target-generation loss and a coherence loss under a gpt-2 surrogate, searched by gradient-guided beam search. Triggered queries land in an empty region of embedding space where only poisoned records live. Agents: Agent-Driver (23k experiences), ReAct on StrategyQA with a 10k-passage Wikipedia base, EHRAgent with 700 added trials (Sec. 4.1). Backbones GPT-3.5 and LLaMA3-70b; embedders DPR, ANCE, BGE, REALM, ORQA and black-box ada-002 for transfer; baselines GCG, AutoDAN, CPA, BadChain. Metrics (Sec. 4.1): ASR-r, all retrieved demonstrations poisoned; ASR-a, target action generated; ASR-t, adversarial effect in the environment; ACC, correct output without trigger.

## Key numbers
- Averages (Sec. 4.2): retrieval success 81.2 percent, target action 59.4 percent, environment impact 62.6 percent, benign impact 0.74 percent. The abstract says at least 80 percent and the contributions list says 82 percent.
- ChatGPT plus contrastive retriever (Table 1): Agent-Driver ASR-r 80.0, ASR-a 68.5, ASR-t 56.8, ACC 91.1 against 91.6 unattacked; ReAct ASR-r 65.5, ASR-a 73.6, ASR-t 58.6, ACC 65.7 against 66.7; EHRAgent ASR-r 98.9, ASR-a 97.9, ASR-t 58.3, ACC 72.9 against 73.0.
- Baseline ASR-r in the same setting: CPA 55.8, 52.8, 96.9; AutoDAN 57.6, 42.9, 84.2 (Table 1).
- Defences, ASR-t under PPL filter and rephrasing (Table 4): AgentPoison 47.2 and 50.0 on Agent-Driver, 61.2 and 62.0 on ReAct; GCG 4.6 and 13.2 on Agent-Driver.
- Rephrasing on EHRAgent (Table 6): ASR-r 34.0, ASR-t 17.0, the one case where a defence bites.
- Transfer across dense retrievers is shown in Figs. 3, 7 and 8 without numbers in the text.

## What Engram borrows
AgentPoison motivates the near-duplicate lint (ADR 0018) and the orphan lint: poisoned demonstrations form a tight, isolated cluster that no benign item neighbours, which a MinHash or native-search neighbourhood check can flag as an outlier set with a shared source. The ASR-r, ASR-a, ASR-t ladder is adopted for the poisoning arms (ADR 0015) because it separates retrieval exposure from adoption from consequence, matching MemSecBench's E1 to E3. The benign-accuracy column is the template for every defence's utility cost.

## What Engram does differently
AgentPoison assumes the attacker can write to the store. Under Engram every write passes the gate with a provenance record; an import of 700 records from one source into a shared scope is a budget and source-kind decision, not a free append (ADR 0007, ADR 0010). Engram does not detect the trigger in the query; it constrains who may add demonstrations to which scope and logs the batch so an outlier cluster is traceable to one actor.

## Risk to our claims
If poisoned records arrive with correct provenance (a trusted document import), the deterministic gate admits them and H1 gains nothing on this attack. Containment then depends on lints and on retrieval explanations showing that all top-k items share one provenance ref, which is observable but not blocking. The thesis should list AgentPoison under "contained by lint, not blocked by gate".

## Open question
Does a rule "no read returns k items that all share one provenance ref unless policy allows" break legitimate single-document recall often enough to be unusable? A step-0 check on LongMemEval would answer it.
