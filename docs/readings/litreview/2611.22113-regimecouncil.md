## Claim
When stored facts go stale and feedback arrives late, neither a purely local nor a purely global rule for spreading negative feedback is sufficient, and a router that arbitrates between the two online beats the better fixed choice.

## Method
The authors formalise "memory conflict governance" under hidden validity and delayed feedback and prove that neither fixed-scope policy suffices. RegimeCouncil keeps a Local Advisor (latest outcome for the queried fact), a Global Advisor (pooled recent outcomes across facts) and a Meta Governor that defaults to local and switches to global only when the pooled recent utility margin is strictly positive, with a finite-time switching guarantee. Paired evidence compares the answer with memory to a no-memory anchor. Tested on LongMemEval update streams, TempLAMA and TAQA with Qwen3-30B-A3B and GPT-4o.

## Key numbers
- Gain over the better fixed-scope policy: 5.2 to 7.3 points on primary disagreement episodes, and better in all 30 held-out settings (abstract).
- Removing any of the three modules lowers accuracy by 2 to 7 points (abstract).
- Only the abstract was read, so table locations are not recorded.

## What Engram borrows
The no-memory anchor as a paired comparator is the same idea as the memory-off condition C1, and supports outcome-driven demotion of items (a validity or trust update) in addition to time-based expiry. A candidate input to the lint set for H3, recorded as an event so the demotion has a reason.

## What Engram does differently
The method learns from outcomes and keeps no logged policy; Engram's expiry and supersession are deterministic and explainable.

## Risk to our claims
It treats staleness as a hidden-state problem that deterministic lints cannot see without feedback, consistent with the keyed versus unkeyed split in H3. No threat beyond reinforcing that split.

## Open question
Can an outcome-feedback signal be recorded as a plane event so a demotion is replayable?
