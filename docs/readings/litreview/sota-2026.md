# State of the field, October 2026

Sweep run on 2026-10-17 over arXiv and venue listings for April to October 2026, restricted to work not already in `docs/readings/`. Sixteen notes with `tier: new` were written beside this file. Numbers below are taken from those notes and cite the same locations.

## Governance has become a paper topic, and two papers now build what Engram proposes

Governed Shared Memory (2606.24535) ships a multi-tenant memory service with four scopes, a trust ladder, provenance chains and asynchronous contradiction detection, and probes it live; its two production bugs (a get-by-id path that skipped the sub-tenant scope check, and a dedup gate that rejected contradictory writes before the detector saw them) show that enforcement has to be tested on every path. What to Admit and How to Present (2610.11188) separates admission from presentation, finds that only admission matters, and reports a 6.7 to 8.8 point pooled failure reduction that comes with a 5 to 8 point personalization loss (Table 2). Authorization Laundering (2609.01836) shows that memory of permissions is itself an attack surface with no attacker: executors act on falsely remembered authority in 98.6 percent of trials (Table 3), and a cited-source gate cuts the laundering from 25.3 to 7.3 percent while cutting legitimate action from 93.3 to 53.8 (Fig. 3). The security survey (2604.16548) formalises five Verifiable Memory Governance primitives (write authorization, provenance visibility, principal-scoped retrieval, rollbackability, verified forgetting), a rubric a reviewer will apply to Engram. The nearest neighbours are 2606.24535 (a plane inside one vendor's store, no utility measurement) and 2610.11188 (deterministic admission, single principal, no event log, no stores). Neither governs several stores, logs decisions as replayable events, or measures a governance tax. The claim "first deterministic policy on memory reads" is gone; "first store-agnostic plane with measured tax" still stands.

## Provenance is now treated as necessary, and the proof is on the write path

Origin-Bound Authority (2606.24322) proves in a bounded TLA+ model that any defence judging content or lineage can be laundered through the agent's own summaries, trusted-tool echoes or planted corroboration, and that binding authority to the write-time origin channel is necessary; its monitor reaches 0 of 4,032 successful attacks across eight models at a decision latency of about 1.3 microseconds against 2,000 ms for an LLM judge (Table II, Sec. VI). Retrieval Admissibility (2610.07309) adds the read side: text-only LLM verifiers cannot detect inadmissible memories at a 1 percent false-denial budget (Fig. 7), while routing by trusted namespace raises anchor recall from 0.432 to 0.533 at 98.3 percent fewer similarity evaluations (Table 2a). With the SAP deployment (2608.00122) storing provenance as governance-only fields, the field has converged on ADR 0004 and 0007: provenance is set by the system at write, never claimed by the model, and the gate is deterministic. What Engram has not written down is that derived items inherit the maximum untrust of their inputs, making `compaction` provenance a trust ceiling.

## Temporal validity and forgetting have benchmarks, and the systems fail them

STALE (2605.06527) shows the best frontier model at 55.2 percent on implicit conflicts and memory frameworks at 5 to 18 percent, with premise resistance near zero for everyone except Gemini-3.1-pro (Table 2); its diagnostic that a framework's update step judges only 3.3 percent of stale top-3 entries as needing an update (Table 3) is the mechanism behind every stale-answer result. MemStrata (2606.26511) and Personal Fact Memory (2610.10265) independently show that a keyed, bi-temporal ledger with deterministic supersession drives stale exposure to zero on templated updates (from 70.3 percent without resolution, 2610.10265 Table 1), and both report the same wall: extraction keys 97 percent of supersessions on templates and 44 percent on messy prose (2606.26511 Sec. 7), and open-domain merge recall on LongMemEval never exceeds 0.062 (2610.10265). Bemula (2610.18052) is the first forgetting benchmark and finds no memory system passes more than 1.5 percent of forget requests end to end. ADR 0005 is vindicated and bounded in the same breath: the expired-validity lint will fire reliably only on keyed items, so H3 must be stated per class and keyed items counted separately.

## Procedural memory has a lifecycle literature, with drift as the central problem

Library Drift (2605.19576, ICML 2026 workshop, Amazon) names the failure where accumulating skills without an outcome signal drives pass rate below the no-skill floor, and shows that outcome-driven retirement with a minimum trial count, a hard cap of 50 active skills and an authoring prior turns +0.002 into +0.328 pass@1 over 100 rounds, at 43 percent more LLM calls and nearly three times the wall time (Table 1, Sec. 6.4). SkillGuard (2605.10990) reframes drift as contract violation: extract role-bearing environment assumptions, check only those, and get zero false positives on 599 negatives and 100 percent precision on known drift, with recall from 24 to 76 percent by backbone (Table 2). ADR 0011's lifecycle matches the field; what the field adds is that retirement needs a trial floor (A4 in 2605.19576 shows harsh retirement is net negative) and that ADR 0011's environment hash should be a contract list.

## Evaluation methodology moved from end-to-end accuracy to per-operation and consumption metrics

VibeMemBench (2609.23570) is the coding-agent memory benchmark the list lacked: 111 executable targets from 90 repositories, verified experience raising resolution by 1.1 to 4.5 points with intervals that all cross zero, and 11 of 12 store-and-solver pairings at or below no memory, with 69.3 percent of failures attributed to the form of the injected record rather than to retrieval (Table 4). The Compliance Trap (2607.10608) measures consumption: agents comply with conflicting memory 63 to 72 percent of the time regardless of scale, success after compliance drops to a 17 to 31 percent floor, and persistent injection is the only schedule that hurts every model (Table 2, Fig. 3). Personal Fact Memory and Retrieval Admissibility both score the memory block before generation. For ADR 0015, memory-off must be a condition on every benchmark: on VibeMemBench and the WebArena pre-check it is already within a few points of the best memory, and a governed store that moves from minus 4 to zero has shown nothing.

## Industry: harness memory is still markdown, and the vendor says it is not enforcement

Claude Code's documentation states that CLAUDE.md and auto memory are "context, not enforced configuration" and directs users to PreToolUse hooks for anything that must hold; auto memory is a per-repository `MEMORY.md` index capped at 200 lines or 25KB, and the reported "auto dream" consolidation pass has no lineage. SAP's shared organizational memory (2608.00122) is the most detailed enterprise write-up: contributor approval at capture, a six-category gate with four outcomes, 900 learnings into 1,144 memories, and tenant isolation, auditable access control and deletion listed as still to do. The Letta governance issue (#3320) and Mem0's harness comparison were thin. No harness or hosted store exposes scope, grants, provenance and validity as enforced, logged fields across stores.

## What changed in the last six months

Between April and October 2026 the field (1) produced its first governance-of-memory systems papers and a VMG rubric, (2) proved write-time origin binding necessary and showed LLM verifiers fail as read gates, (3) shipped benchmarks for implicit staleness (STALE), forgetting (Bemula), authorization memory (EAL-Bench) and coding-agent memory (VibeMemBench), (4) converged on keyed bi-temporal supersession and documented its extraction ceiling, and (5) made skill-library drift a measured failure with two detector designs. Threats: 2606.24535 and 2610.11188. Baselines: MemGate (as reimplemented in 2610.11188), MemStrata, PFM's participant-aware BM25, SkillGuard. Benchmarks to pin, in order: VibeMemBench, STALE, EAL-Bench, Bemula, MEM-INV-Bench.
