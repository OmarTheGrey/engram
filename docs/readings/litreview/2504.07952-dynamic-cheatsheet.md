## Claim
A black-box model given a persistent, curated memory of strategies and code snippets at inference time improves sharply on some reasoning tasks, with no weight updates and no ground-truth labels, but only when the model is strong enough to curate its own memory.

## Method
Two variants (Sec. 2). DC-Cu (cumulative): the generator answers with the current memory, then a curator, the same model, updates the memory. DC-RS (retrieval and synthesis): retrieve the top-3 most similar past input-output pairs by text-embedding-3-small, curate memory with them, then answer. The curator judges correctness itself, adds useful entries, revises or removes faulty ones, and consolidates toward a target of about 2,000 to 2,500 words. Baselines: plain prompting (BL), DC with empty memory (DC-0), dynamic retrieval without curation (DR), and full-history appending (FH). Models: GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku, with DeepSeek R1 and o1 in extra runs. Benchmarks: AIME 2020 to 2024 (133), AIME 2024 (30), AIME 2025 (30), GPQA-Diamond (198), Game of 24 (100), Math Equation Balancer (250), MMLU-Pro Engineering and Physics (250 each).

## Key numbers
- Game of 24, GPT-4o: 10.0 percent (BL) to 99.0 (DC-RS); DC-0 reaches 19.0. Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 12.0 to 14.0 (Table 1).
- AIME 2024, Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 23.3 to 50.0 (DC-Cu); AIME 2025: 6.7 to 36.7; AIME 2020 to 2024: 6.7 to 40.6 (DC-RS) (Table 1).
- AIME 2024, GPT-4o: 20.0 to 40.0 (DC-RS); AIME 2025: 6.7 to 20.0 (Table 1).
- GPQA-Diamond, Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 59.6 to 68.7 (DC-RS); GPT-4o 57.1 to 58.1, with Table 1 and the text disagreeing on which variant gives which (Table 1, Sec. 3).
- Math Equation Balancer: Claude 44.8 to 100 (DC-Cu); GPT-4o 50.0 to 100 (DC-Cu) (Table 1).
- Full history is worse than curated memory: AIME 2024 Claude 26.7 (FH) against 50.0 (DC-Cu); GPT-4o 6.7 (FH) against 40.0 (DC-RS) (Table 2).
- Majority voting matches baseline at 23.3 on AIME 2024 while DC-Cu reaches 50.0 (Table 4).
- Smaller models gain little or inconsistently (Table 3). Tokens: Claude on AIME 2024 averages 370 under BL and 1,831 under DC-Cu (Sec. 4).

## What Engram borrows
Two protocol points and one rule. Full-history memory loses to curated memory (Table 2), which is the cleanest published support for lints and retirement over unbounded accumulation (ADR 0008, ADR 0010). Small models poison their own memory (Table 3), which is why ADR 0016 insists on three models from three providers and reports H5 per provider rather than pooled. The curator's word budget becomes a per-scope retention budget expressed in tokens (ADR 0010).

## What Engram does differently
Dynamic Cheatsheet lets the model write, judge and prune its own memory with no external check. Engram's write gate and lints are deterministic and model-free at the gate (ADR 0007), the plane never calls a model (rule 4), and no item is removed, only retired by event. Engram stores procedures induced from sessions, not solution sketches curated per benchmark item.

## Risk to our claims
The 10 to 99 percent jump on Game of 24 comes from one stored Python snippet, and the same memory does almost nothing for Claude (12 to 14). Memory effects can be one trick on one model. If H5's governed procedure gain is driven by one procedure on one provider, a pooled interval would overstate it. Mitigation: per-provider reporting, and D4 reports the distribution of utility across procedures, not only the mean.

## Open question
The curator's "abbreviate instead of rewrite" failure degrades memory silently (Sec. 4). Is there a deterministic lint for that, such as a shrinking-content-hash check on compaction entries, which ADR 0004 already marks as lowest trust?
