## Claim
Language models use information at the start and end of a long input far better than information in the middle; the accuracy curve over position is U-shaped, and in the worst position a model given retrieved documents can score below its own closed-book answer.

## Method
Multi-document QA on 2,655 NaturalQuestions-Open queries whose long answer is a paragraph; contexts hold 10, 20 or 30 Wikipedia passages of at most 100 tokens, exactly one containing the answer, with its position varied (Sec. 2.1, 2.3). A synthetic key-value task uses JSON objects of 75, 140 or 300 UUID pairs, 500 examples each (Sec. 3.1). Models: MPT-30B-Instruct, LongChat-13B (16K), GPT-3.5-Turbo and 16K, Claude-1.3 and 100K, GPT-4 on a 500-example subset (Sec. 2.2, Appendix D). Ablations: query-aware contextualisation, instruction fine-tuning, encoder-decoder models (Sec. 4). Section 5 compares retriever recall against reader accuracy as retrieved documents grow.

## Key numbers
- Closed-book / oracle: GPT-3.5-Turbo 56.1 / 88.3; Claude-1.3 48.3 / 76.1; LongChat-13B 35.0 / 83.4; MPT-30B-Instruct 31.5 / 81.9 (Table 1).
- 20 documents, best vs worst position: GPT-3.5-Turbo 75.8 at index 0 vs 53.8 at index 9; LongChat-13B 68.6 vs 52.5; Claude-1.3 60.1 vs 55.9; MPT-30B-Instruct 56.3 vs 51.8 (Appendix G.2, Table 6).
- 30 documents: GPT-3.5-Turbo (16K) 73.4 at index 0, 50.5 at index 9, below its 56.1 closed-book score (Table 7, Sec. 2.3).
- Key-value retrieval without query-aware contextualisation: worst case 45.6 percent; with it, near perfect (Figure 7, Sec. 4.2).
- Instruction fine-tuning narrows the worst-case gap from nearly 10 points to about 4 (Sec. 4.3).
- Reader accuracy saturates before recall: 20 to 50 documents adds about 1.5 points for GPT-3.5-Turbo and about 1 for Claude-1.3 (Sec. 5).

## What Engram borrows
Rank is part of the outcome. ADR 0009 requires every `Plane.read` explanation to carry the item's rank and position in the injected context, because a governed recall that admits the right item and places it ninth of twenty may be worse than returning nothing. The read filter in the pi `context` handler orders admitted items by policy priority and places the highest-priority items at the start and end of the block, a direct application of the U-curve. The saturation result is why the retention budget (ADR 0010) also caps items per read: more than about 20 buys nothing and costs tokens, which is the H2 tax.

## What Engram does differently
This paper studies a reader given a fixed context. Engram controls what enters the context and where, so position becomes a policy variable the simulator can replay. The paper has no notion of an item that is correct but inadmissible; Engram's explanation says why an item was left out, not only where an admitted one sat.

## Risk to our claims
H3 claims lints recover part of the governance loss. If part of the loss is positional (admitted items pushed to the middle by explanation text and policy preambles), lints cannot recover it and the H2 knee is partly an artefact of where Engram places its own metadata. Mitigation: the injected explanation is kept short and placed at the end of the block; the X-cards include a run with explanations logged but not injected, so the positional cost is measured.

## Open question
These models are from 2023. Do the three 2026 hosted models in Engram's grid still show a U-curve at 40K tokens of injected memory, and should step 0 run the key-value probe through pi-ai on each before policy decides placement?
