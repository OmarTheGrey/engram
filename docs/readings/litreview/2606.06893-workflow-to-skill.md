## Claim
Note first: the readings list calls this a survey. It is a method paper. It argues that building a skill from traces is not summarization, because traces are fragmented, redundant and can omit rare safety-critical behaviour, and that a skill should be decomposed into a routing header, a workflow backbone, operational semantics and runtime attachments before it is written.

## Method
RWSA is an intermediate representation (Sec. 3): R is the routing header that decides when the skill is selected; W is a graph of execution units and directed links, counted as a workflow only with at least two nodes and one edge; S holds node-level objectives, decision logic and success or failure criteria; A holds tools, resources, constraints, state operations, validation checks and output schemas. Eight skill types T0 to T7 are defined by which of W, S, A are present (T0 prompt fragment, T4 bare workflow, T7 full runtime workflow). W2S parses traces into WSA evidence, enumerates W-paths, drafts a local skill per path, aligns shared structure, reconciles branches, compresses redundancy while keeping evidence and confidence annotations, then runs a feedback loop with three checks: coverage (missing segments, branches, criteria, attachments), consistency (contradictions between workflow and attachments), executability (ordering, placement of criteria, specificity of validation and outputs), until no high-priority error remains or a budget is spent (Sec. 5). The WSASkill benchmark has 70 reference skills across the eight types, 10 traces per W-path, and replay-based behavioural fidelity as the metric. The only baseline is Anthropic Skill Creator (ASC). No models are named.

## Key numbers
- Replay score, W2S against ASC: T0 0.623 / 0.520, T1 0.522 / 0.407, T2 0.760 / 0.638, T3 0.573 / 0.533, T4 0.276 / 0.227, T5 0.480 / 0.550, T6 0.515 / 0.450, T7 0.652 / 0.613; average 0.503 / 0.455 (Table 3).
- The abstract's 10.5 percent is the relative gain of the averages, 0.048 over 0.455.
- The reported averages are not the unweighted means of the rows; per-type counts are not given.
- No variance, significance test, ablation or model name is reported.

## What Engram borrows
The W, S, A split is a checklist for the procedure item schema (ADR 0002): ordered steps, a success criterion per step, and the tools and constraints a step needs. The three feedback checks become deterministic candidate lints before replay (ADR 0008): a candidate with a step that names no success criterion, or that references a tool not present in the inducing session, is flagged before any replay budget is spent. "Traces may miss rare but safety-critical behaviour" is the argument for keeping abandoned branches in the induction input (ADR 0012).

## What Engram does differently
Engram does not adopt RWSA as a format; procedures stay in AWM's text form so the induction method is a published one, not a new one. W2S builds skills offline from curated traces; Engram induces from live pi sessions and governs the result through a lifecycle, which W2S does not have.

## Risk to our claims
T5, workflows with attachments but no semantics, is where W2S loses to ASC (0.480 against 0.550, Table 3). Coding procedures are attachment-heavy (tools, paths, environment variables). If induction from traces is weakest exactly on the kind of procedure pi sessions produce, the candidate pool for H5 will be low quality before governance touches it. Mitigation: record the share of candidates rejected by the attachment lint and by replay, so a weak pool is visible as a Distill finding, not hidden in H5.

## Open question
Is replay-based behavioural fidelity against a reference skill a usable admission metric when there is no reference, only held-out tasks? Probably not, but the five fidelity facets (progression, decisions, tool constraints, validation, final response) are worth borrowing as judge rubric lines.
