## Claim
Deleting an original memory record can leave its derived descendants (summaries, composed facts) intact, and a model can recover the deleted secret from them without any verbatim copy. Deletion must be judged at the level of agent plus memory system, not the storage layer.

## Method
Synthetic secrets are injected into a fixed-weight agent and then deleted. Each exposed-then-deleted agent is compared with a matched never-exposed control, so any recovery is attributable to the exposure. The paper tests whether recovery needs a surviving verbatim copy, whether response format distorts scoring, and whether provenance-aware closure or an exact-literal write filter prevents recovery.

## Key numbers
- A single query recovers the deleted secret from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in about two thirds of cases; never-exposed controls recover at zero (abstract page).
- When only separately stored components remain under a public composition rule, exact reconstruction succeeds in 62 to 99 percent of cases across Qwen, Mistral and Phi-3.
- Provenance-aware closure eliminated observed recovery in the tested positive-baseline settings; an exact-literal write filter blocks reinsertion but cannot erase what is present.
- Only the abstract page was read; per-table numbers were not read.

## What Engram borrows
The matched exposed-versus-never-exposed control as the shape of an erasure test for offboarding (extends ADR 0010's erase). Provenance-aware closure maps onto Engram's provenance refs: erase must cascade to every item whose provenance names an erased one (card AB-TWN-1).

## What Engram does differently
Engram deletes the person scope's items and redacts the log, and sweeps store records whose lineage names an erased id (offboard.ts), but has no closure over promoted items, which stay by the lead's decision 38a of 2026-11-20. The paper's benchmark is behavioural on a model; ours would be a replay check on the plane.

## Risk to our claims
Offboarding's deletion report claims erasure of the person scope, but promoted or distilled items derived from it may carry the content. If so the report overstates erasure. Mitigation: closure over provenance in the erase, and report derived items by id.

## Open question
Does an item promoted from person to team scope with a provenance ref to the leaver's item count as derived content that offboarding must retire or flag?
