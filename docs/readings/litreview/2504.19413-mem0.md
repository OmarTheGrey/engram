## Claim
A two-phase pipeline, extract candidate facts from each new exchange then let an LLM choose ADD, UPDATE, DELETE or NOOP against the most similar stored memories, beats full-context and RAG on LoCoMo at a fraction of the tokens and latency. A graph variant helps on temporal and open-domain questions at extra cost.

## Method
Extraction takes the latest message pair, a rolling summary and the last m = 10 messages and asks GPT-4o-mini for candidate facts; the summary refreshes asynchronously (Section 2.1). Update retrieves the top s = 10 similar memories and the model picks one of four operations via a tool call (Algorithm 1, Appendix B). Mem0g stores entities and labelled triples in Neo4j, marks conflicting edges invalid rather than deleting them, and retrieves by entity expansion or triple similarity (2.2). Evaluation on LoCoMo, 10 conversations of about 26k tokens, four categories, adversarial excluded (3.1); F1, BLEU-1 and an LLM judge J averaged over 10 runs (3.2); baselines A-Mem, LangMem, Zep, OpenAI memory, RAG at chunk sizes 128 to 8192 and full context (3.3).

## What Engram borrows
- ADD, UPDATE, DELETE, NOOP is the interception point for the Mem0 adapter (ADR 0007 and 0013): each operation becomes a gated write event; DELETE and UPDATE become supersede events under ADR 0005 so Mem0's in-place semantics are preserved in the store but versioned in the plane.
- Mem0g's invalidate-not-delete rule is store-native validity; the adapter's `native_validity` flag exists for it.
- Table 2's columns (J, p50, p95, tokens) are the deployment metrics in the pre-registration; H2's governance tax is the delta on exactly these.

## What Engram does differently
Mem0 decides what to store with a model call and gives no reason, no provenance and no scope; a fact from the user and a fact the extractor invented are both ADDed. Engram does not call a model on the write path, records why each operation was allowed, and attaches provenance to the candidate. Mem0 can be driven from a shell or SDK; Engram's no-bypass rule (ADR 0014) wraps that path.

## Risk to our claims
Full-context scores 72.90 J against Mem0's 66.88 (Table 2). If a governed Mem0 pays a further tax (H2), the gap to the no-memory long-context condition widens and H3's recovery has to be large to matter. We keep full-context as a condition and report H2 and H3 against both the ungoverned store and the long-context baseline.

## Open question
The judge is "a separate, more capable LLM" (3.2) and is not named. Our judge vendoring rule (ADR 0015) cannot pin what the paper did not state; do we re-run Mem0 with our own pinned judge and accept that our Mem0 number will not match the paper's?
