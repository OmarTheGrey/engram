## Claim
A training platform acting as a neutral observer can generate a signed AI-BOM for every training job, capturing datasets, model metadata and environment, and detect tampering with artifacts at negligible overhead.

## Method
Per the abstract: the platform enforces AIBOM creation, with cryptographic hashing, digital signatures and in-toto attestations as integrity protection.

## Key numbers
None stated in the sources read (the abstract says "negligible" overhead without a figure).

## What Engram borrows
The pattern of a neutral generator that signs the inventory: the AI-BOM for an Engram deployment (AB-CMP-4) is generated by the plane from the deployment's configuration and log, not authored by the operator, and wrapped in an in-toto/DSSE statement.

## What Engram does differently
AIBoMGen covers training. Engram does not train; its AI-BOM lists models called through pi-ai (by provider/model id and cassette hash), stores, adapters, policy hash, schema version and benchmark pins.

## Risk to our claims
Without a training pipeline the BOM may look thin. It is still useful because the model-call layer already records every model id and prompt hash (ADR 0017).

## Open question
Do deployers want models listed by observed use in the log or by configuration?
