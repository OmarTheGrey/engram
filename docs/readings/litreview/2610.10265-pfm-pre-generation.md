## Claim
Judging personal memory only by the final answer hides three failures that happen before generation: a superseded value in the prompt, a fact about the wrong person, and no useful fact arriving by the deadline. With a keyed, bi-temporal fact store, serving only the active value removes stale exposure entirely on a controlled revision benchmark (from 70.3 percent without update resolution), a simple participant-aware BM25 matches the reference ranker within a preset margin, and the hard remaining problem is assigning a revision to the right slot, where open-domain merge recall on LongMemEval never exceeds 0.062.

## Method
Personal Fact Memory (PFM) is a reference layer: each fact has text, entities, participants, an optional slot key, an activity interval and a last-observed time (Def. 1, Sec. 3); a new fact with the same key closes its active predecessor atomically and keeps it as history (Prop. 1). Keyless drafts are absorbed only when an active fact shares an entity, has token Jaccard at least 0.8 and the same value-bearing tokens (Sec. 4). Serving is BM25F over text, entity and participant fields plus recency, usage and a one-hop association term, with no embedding or model on the path (Sec. 4). An identity posterior scores candidate entities with participant, graph and recency terms (Eq. 2, Sec. 5). Two abstention gates, a schema-aware slot gate and a schema-free margin gate. Controlled benchmark: 160 revision chains per instance with 0 to 4 revisions, 1,000 filler messages, 5 seeds, 1,200 queries (Sec. 6). Public corpora: LongMemEval knowledge-update questions and LoCoMo same-first-name speakers. Rankers: BM25, BM25 plus validity, plus participants, Dense (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), Hybrid (RRF). Key assigners: a rule extractor and four model assigners from 3B to 14B (qwen2.5 7B and 14B named), plus a Mem0-shaped decision (Sec. 7.2). Two frozen LMs reproduce prompt errors in generated text.

## Key numbers
- Stale exposure: keyless store 70.3 percent; keyed store 0.000, bounded at or below 0.005 (Table 1, Sec. 7.1).
- Ranker equivalence: BM25 plus validity plus participants 0.873 against PFM 0.877, difference 0.005, 90 percent CI minus 0.008 to 0.019, TOST p 0.038 at a preset margin of 0.02 (Sec. 7.1, Sec. 6).
- Open-domain merge recall on LongMemEval at or below 0.062 (abstract, Sec. 1).
- Model key assigners: key recall 0.980 to 0.996 against 0.807 for rules, but clean retrieval 0.560 to 0.644 against 0.781 for rules (Table 10, Sec. 7.2).
- Missed merges raise stale exposure from 0.100 at p=0.05 to 0.370 at p=0.2 to 0.592 at p=0.4; false merges lose the current value on 6.1 percent of queries at q=0.05 and 22.5 percent at q=0.2 (Sec. 7.2, Table 16).
- Coarse key drops current-value recall to 0.500; 30 percent out-of-order delivery lowers clean retrieval by 0.130 (Sec. 7.2).
- Misattribution: wrong-person rate 0.121 for PFM, all 145 cases on the 223 ambiguous queries; identity posterior reduces it to 0.032; LoCoMo identically named speakers remain indistinguishable (Table 3, Sec. 7.1).
- Latency: PFM p50 0.25 ms, p99 0.69 ms; Dense p50 18.3 ms, p99 93.0 ms; Hybrid p50 18.2 ms, p99 82.4 ms; prefill dominates the turn (Table 2).

## What Engram borrows
The pre-generation metric set, current-value recall, stale exposure, misattribution, abstention and clean retrieval by deadline (Sec. 1, Eq. 1), is the right per-operation evaluation for the lints in H3 and belongs in ADR 0015 beside HaluMem's operation-level scoring. "Stale exposure" is the direct measure of the expired-validity lint (ADR 0008, L1) and should be reported for every condition, not only accuracy. The participant field and the identity posterior are the twin's missing piece (ADR 0021): an item's `owner` is who it is about as well as who wrote it, and a misattribution lint is needed when two people share a name in one scope. The finding that model key assigners have high key recall but lower clean retrieval than rules (Table 10) supports ADR 0018's rule-first stance and says the model-based tier must be measured on clean retrieval, not only on recall.

## What Engram does differently
PFM is a single-user personal store; Engram adds scope and grants, so "wrong person" is both a misattribution lint and a potential invariant 1 violation. PFM requires a slot inventory to key facts; Engram leaves the key optional and treats keying as a provenance-time enrichment that lints can revisit (ADR 0004, 0005). Engram also records the superseding event with reason and policy, which PFM's atomic commit does not need.

## Risk to our claims
Merge recall of 0.062 on open-domain LongMemEval means that without slots, almost no revision gets attached to the fact it supersedes, so the expired-validity lint has nothing to fire on. H3 depends on the fraction of items Engram can key. The mitigation is the same as for MemStrata: key structurally where possible (tool results, file paths, config), report keyed and unkeyed stale exposure separately, and state H3 per class.

## Open question
Their equivalence test shows a deterministic ranker matches the reference once validity and participant filters are applied. Does Engram's read filter on a store's native search give the same result, and is that a cheap way to show that governance does not cost retrieval quality (H2) on personal memory?
