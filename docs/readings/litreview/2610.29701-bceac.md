## Claim
A policy engine's allow does not show that every required governance source was consulted or that the request context was exactly the one the policy judged. Admission needs a separate verifier of those bindings.

## Method
Defines the Binding-Complete Execution Admission Contract: governance resolution, policy evaluation, independent admission verification and execution mediation as separate stages, working across engines that meet declared profiles. Tested on a finite governance-source completeness study and an offline review of archived OPA and Cedar records. Only the abstract was read.

## Key numbers
- 72 scenarios, 144 strict case-engine records (120 native-invoked, 24 pre-native), 8 permissive controls excluded (abstract).
- 74 pages. The authors say results are bounded and give no machine-checked safety theorem.

## What Engram borrows
The binding check: an allow event carries the policy id and a hash, plus the actor and item snapshot it judged, so replay can verify the decision was made under the policy in force. Informs the diff report in AB-POL-5 and a possible `policy_hash` on events (a schema change, its own PR under rule 5).

## What Engram does differently
Engram's plane is one process with one evaluator, so the stages collapse; the log, replayed through the oracle, is the independent verifier.

## Risk to our claims
Argues "policy engine plus log" is insufficient without binding completeness. Engram's replay check answers it only if events record the policy version used.

## Open question
Do MemoryEvents record enough to rebuild the exact policy a decision used?
