## Claim
A memory agent that escalates failed items through a cost-ordered cascade (teacher model, tool-augmented teacher, simulated expert) and retrieves memories with a utility-aware Thompson sampler beats prior memory baselines and matches or beats GRPO fine-tuning at roughly half the training time.

## Method
U-Mem runs each training item; a correct run yields procedural memory directly, while a failure escalates through Level 1 (teacher LLM, Gemini-3.0-flash), Level 2 (teacher with a Python interpreter) and Level 3 (a simulated expert, Gemini-3-pro-preview) (Sec. 3.2 to 3.3, Figure 2). Each memory carries a Gaussian utility initialised from its 10 nearest neighbours; retrieval scores (1 minus lambda) times similarity plus lambda times sampled utility with lambda 0.1, and utilities update from the advantage of memory-augmented over base performance (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 1 to 5). New memories are appended, merged or pruned. Benchmarks: AIME (943 train, 30 test), HotpotQA (2,000 train, 500 test), AdvancedIF and HelpSteer3 STEM judged by gpt-5.1. Models: Qwen2.5-7B and 14B-Instruct, Gemini-2.5-flash, DeepSeek-chat. Baselines: no memory, ReasoningBank, ReMe, MemRL, GRPO (Sec. 4.1, Tables 1, 2, 5).

## Key numbers
- HotpotQA, Qwen2.5-7B: 37.80 to 52.40, +14.6; GRPO 46.60 (Table 2).
- AIME25, Gemini-2.5-flash: 46.67 to 54.00, +7.33 (Table 5).
- AIME25, Qwen2.5-14B: U-Mem 18.67 plus or minus 1.83 vs MemRL 10.00 and no memory 12.00 (Table 2).
- Expert (Level 3) calls on HotpotQA with Qwen2.5-7B: 23.17 percent of cases (Table 3); no fractions for Levels 1 and 2.
- Training time vs GRPO: AIME 10.5 h vs 19.5 h; AdvancedIF 2.7 vs 9.7; STEM 9.3 vs 17.6 (Appendix B.1, Figure 6).
- Gain tracks task similarity, Pearson r 0.888 (Sec. 4.4); performance drops when retrieved k exceeds 3 (Table 4).

## What Engram borrows
Two mechanisms map onto Distill. The per-memory utility with an advantage update is a published form of the smoothed success rate ADR 0011 attaches to active procedures, and merge-or-prune is a lifecycle event Engram logs rather than performs silently. The cost-ordered cascade is a model for how procedure validation in the D-cards spends calls: deterministic replay first, a model-based check second, human review last, each an event with its cost. The similarity finding (r 0.888) supports retirement on environment-hash change: a procedure mined for one task structure should not be offered on another.

## What Engram does differently
U-Mem's agent writes its own memory with no gate and consolidates in place; Engram requires admission by replay and keeps every superseded version. U-Mem's "expert" is a stronger model, so the cascade is distillation; Engram's human step is a person. U-Mem reports no dollar cost; Engram meters every call (ADR 0017).

## Risk to our claims
Ungoverned, self-curated memory with a utility prior beats RL here. If an ungoverned procedural store with a simple utility score already captures most of the gain, H5's admission step may add cost without accuracy. Mitigation: H5 includes an ungoverned procedural condition with utility-weighted retrieval as a baseline, not only no-memory, and reports admission cost separately.

## Open question
Retrieval degrades past k 3. Is that Lost in the Middle or procedures interfering with each other, and does an explanation of why each was offered change how many the agent follows?
