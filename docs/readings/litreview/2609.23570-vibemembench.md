## Claim
Repository history contains experience that, when injected in a verified form, raises executable task resolution by 1 to 4.5 points and shortens trajectories on every solver; but when four existing memory systems must build and retrieve that experience themselves, 11 of 12 solver-and-system pairings do no better than no memory, and the dominant failure is not retrieval but the form in which the record is rendered.

## Method
111 coding targets from 90 SWE-rebench V2 repositories, with 3,634 completed history trajectories (Sec. 3.1). A target is kept only if injecting a distilled experience improves its executable outcome in a reference setting (deepseek-v4-flash with MiniSWEAgent, four seeds per condition), so every target has an experience whose usefulness was verified by execution (Sec. 3.3). The SIEVE pipeline does source validation, patch-pattern screening, history execution with experience distillation, and retention by uplift (Sec. 3.2). Bug fixes are under a third of labels; the rest are feature, interface, configuration and test-infrastructure work (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 3). Transfer solvers: deepseek-v4-pro, glm-5, glm-5.2, kimi-k2.7-code, qwen3.8-max. Memory systems: Mem0, SimpleMem, MemoryOS, A-MEM, with deepseek-v4-flash for extraction and Qwen3-Embedding-4B for retrieval; three solvers for this arm (Sec. 3.5). Retrieval is pre-trajectory, not online. Failures are attributed over 231 failing pairings on 85 targets with LLM judgment (Table 4).

## Key numbers
- Frozen verified experience, Resolved off to on: deepseek-v4-pro 67.1 to 67.1 (+0.0); glm-5 55.0 to 57.4 (+2.4); glm-5.2 76.8 to 80.4 (+3.6); kimi-k2.7-code 69.8 to 74.3 (+4.5); qwen3.8-max 79.1 to 80.2 (+1.1); all five 95 percent paired bootstrap CIs cross zero; steps fall on all five, by 2.2 to 10.9 (Table 1, Sec. 4.2).
- Irrelevant-memory control lowers Resolved by 0.7 to 3.4 points on three solvers (Table 1).
- Memory systems, delta in points against memory off: deepseek-v4-pro Mem0 minus 4.3, SimpleMem minus 0.2, MemoryOS minus 2.5, A-MEM minus 4.0; glm-5 minus 5.5, minus 1.8, +2.0, minus 2.5; kimi-k2.7-code minus 1.8, minus 0.2, minus 3.8, minus 4.0; 11 of 12 pairings at or below baseline (Table 2, Sec. 4.2).
- Net effect across 1,332 retrieval pairings: a loss of 127 resolved seeds (Sec. 4.2).
- Failure attribution: form degradation 69.3 percent (160), coverage miss 16.0 (37), use failure 13.4 (31), ranking miss 1.3 (3); injected records average 65 lines and about half trigger instruction-pollution markers (Table 4, Sec. 4.4).
- 42.0 percent of retrieval pairings sit at the memory-off ceiling of 4 of 4 seeds, where a record can only lose; 64 of 118 opposite-sign pairs show the same record helping a weaker solver and hurting a stronger one (Table 3, Sec. 4.3).
- Mem0 dose curve from one to five records stays below baseline at every budget (App. O).
- Attribution inter-rater agreement: Cohen's kappa 0.24 to 0.35 (limitations).

## What Engram borrows
VibeMemBench is the missing coding-agent benchmark for ADR 0015: executable outcome, real repositories, and a memory-off baseline that is already strong. It should replace or sit beside MemoryAgentBench for the pi-native condition because pi is a coding agent. The target selection rule (keep only targets where verified experience helps) is a protocol Engram can reuse for the procedural arm (D-series): a procedure is a candidate only if its replay improves a held-out target (ADR 0011). The form-degradation finding is a design input for ADR 0009: what the plane injects must be the distilled fix pattern, not transcript, and the explanation should carry the record's length and source kind so the "instruction pollution" risk is visible. The opposite-sign finding (same record helps a weak solver, hurts a strong one) is an argument for reporting H4 per model rather than pooled, which ADR 0016 already requires.

## What Engram does differently
VibeMemBench measures stores that both extract and retrieve; Engram governs what a store extracts (write gate, provenance, class) and what reaches the prompt (read filter, explanation), and its hypothesis is that governance reduces exactly the form and pollution failures they attribute 69 percent of losses to, by refusing `compaction` and `model` items without a gate pass (ADR 0004, 0007). Engram also measures memory-side cost (ADR 0017), which the paper leaves out.

## Risk to our claims
Even verified, hand-picked experience gives gains whose intervals all cross zero at 111 targets (Table 1), and four stores are net negative. If governance on top of Mem0 moves the result from minus 4.3 to zero, H2 reports a tax on a memory that was already a loss, and H5's net-positive claim for procedures has to be shown against the memory-off baseline, not against ungoverned memory. The plan is to make memory-off a required condition on this benchmark and to state H5 as "governed procedures beat no memory", which is the harder claim.

## Open question
Form degradation dominates and ranking misses are 1.3 percent. Does a plane that only filters and explains (no rewriting) change form at all, or does Engram need a distillation step at write time, which would be a model call inside the governed path and would move the H1 enforcement condition?
