## Claim
Agent memory can be described by three functions, writing W, management P and reading R, over three sources (inside-trial, cross-trial, external knowledge) in two forms (textual, parametric). As of early 2024 there was no open benchmark aimed at the memory module itself.

## Method
A survey that claims to be the first on the topic (Section 1). Section 3 defines memory narrowly (same-trial history) and broadly (plus cross-trial and external sources) and gives the W, P, R formulation (3.4). Section 5 tabulates 28 systems by source (Table 1), form (Table 2) and operation (Table 3), with textual forms split into complete, recent, retrieved and external, and parametric forms into fine-tuning and editing. Section 6 splits evaluation into direct (subjective coherence and rationality; objective correctness, reference accuracy, time and hardware cost) and indirect task-based. Section 7 covers seven application areas. Section 8 lists four future directions. No experiments.

## Key numbers
- Tables 1 and 2, Section 5: 28 systems summarised; 28 use inside-trial information, 6 cross-trial, 13 external knowledge.
- Section 6.3: the authors state there are no open-source benchmarks tailored to agent memory modules (as of April 2024).
- No other numbers stated in the sources read.

## What Engram borrows
- The W, P, R split is a clean statement of where a plane can sit: the gate wraps W, the lints and retention are a governed P, the read filter wraps R. The glossary's they-say-we-say table uses these letters.
- Section 6.1.2's objective metrics, especially "reference accuracy" (F1 over retrieved entries) and "time and hardware cost," are two of the columns the pre-registration reports per condition (S0.12), and they predate the cost-accounting arguments in later work.
- Section 5.3.1's point that extraction strategy matters because raw observations are long and noisy supports treating extraction output as `kind: model` provenance (ADR 0004).
- Table 3's operation inventory per system is a seed for the fault fixtures in L2: each writing or management behaviour is a place a seeded fault can enter.

## What Engram does differently
The survey has no notion of a principal: memory is the agent's. It mentions privacy and compliance once (Section 5.2.1, about external APIs) and does not treat access, provenance or deletion as design dimensions. Engram's scope, owner and grants (ADR 0006) and its event log are outside this survey's frame, which is fair since the paper predates multi-principal shared stores.

## Risk to our claims
Section 6.3 says indirect evaluation conflates memory with other factors. H3 claims lints recover part of a loss; if the loss and the recovery are measured only on task score, the attribution is exactly the conflation this survey warns about. The mitigation is already in ADR 0008: every lint finding is an event, so recovery is measured first as findings on seeded faults (precision and recall per lint) and only second as task score.

## Open question
The survey counts reading a memory as "usually similarity to the current context" (3.4). If Engram's read filter removes the top similarity hit on policy grounds, does the explanation need to say what was removed, and to whom?
