## Claim
Operational mappings from AI Act requirements to concrete verification activities are scarce. The paper decomposes requirements into sub-requirements, grounds each in standards, and characterises verification activities by type and lifecycle stage.

## Method
Abstract only was read: requirement decomposition, standards grounding, and a two-dimensional characterisation (verification type by lifecycle stage), offered as a technology-agnostic reference.

## Key numbers
None stated in the sources read.

## What Engram borrows
The method for the thesis crosswalk (AB-CMP-5): each governance claim is decomposed to a sub-requirement and tied to a verification activity that a test or a report in this repository performs. The structure, not the contents, is borrowed.

## What Engram does differently
The mapping targets whole high-risk systems. Engram maps only what a memory control plane can evidence (logging, traceability, human oversight inputs, data governance of memory content) and states what it cannot (accuracy, robustness of the model).

## Risk to our claims
A crosswalk invites the claim "Engram makes you compliant". The thesis must say it supplies evidence for specific obligations, not conformity.

## Open question
Which of the paper's activity types are automatable from an event log?
