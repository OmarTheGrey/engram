## Claim
An agent that induces reusable workflows from its own past trajectories and keeps them in text memory solves more web tasks in fewer steps than an agent given fixed examples, and the gain grows as the test distribution moves away from training.

## Method
A workflow is a textual description plus a sequence of steps; each step has a natural-language description of the environment state, the agent's reasoning, and an executable action (Sec. 2.2, Figure 2 example "find a customer order with specified ID"). LM-based induction uses one prompt (App. A.1) that opens "Given a list of web navigation tasks, your task is to extract the common workflows", asks for repeated action subsets of at least two steps, no overlapping workflows, and non-fixed values replaced by descriptive variable names such as {product-name}; output is split on double line breaks (Sec. 2.3). A rule-based alternative (App. B) deduplicates by action sequence and drops non-executable steps. Offline mode induces from annotated training examples; online mode induces from the agent's own test-time trajectories that the LM evaluator of Pan et al. 2024 labels correct (Sec. 2.3, Sec. 3.2.1). WebArena: 812 tasks, gpt-4-0613 at temperature 0, baselines BrowserGym, AutoEval, SteP (14 human-written workflows). Mind2Web: MindAct, Synapse, CogAgent with gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4; the same model induces and acts (Sec. 3).

## Key numbers
- WebArena success: AWM 35.5, SteP 33.0, BrowserGym 23.5, AutoEval 20.2, WebArena 14.9; steps AWM 5.9 against AutoEval 46.7 (Table 1).
- Relative gains: 51.1 percent over BrowserGym, 7.9 percent over SteP; up to 22.5 points after tens of examples (Sec. 1, Figure 1).
- Mind2Web cross-task, gpt-4, element accuracy / action F1 / step SR / task SR: MindAct 41.6 / 60.6 / 36.2 / 2.0; AWM 50.6 / 57.3 / 45.1 / 4.8 (Table 3); 24.6 percent relative step SR (Sec. 1).
- Cross-domain step SR, gpt-4: MindAct 18.6, AWM online 35.5; cross-website 30.1 against 33.9 (Table 4). Abstract: 8.9 to 14.0 absolute points as the gap widens.
- Rule-based against LM induction on WebArena: 35.6 against 35.5 (Table 5).
- About 7.4 workflows induced per run, utility rate 0.94 (WebArena) and 0.91 (Mind2Web) (Table 10).
- Offline plus online is worse than online alone on cross-domain step SR: 34.1 against 35.5 (Table 11).

## What Engram borrows
ADR 0012 fixes this induction method; the App. A.1 prompt is vendored verbatim. The workflow format becomes the procedure item: description plus ordered (state, reasoning, action) steps. Online mode is the Distill loop over pi sessions (D2); the evaluator slot becomes replay validation (D3). Table 10's utility rate becomes a D4 metric. Step count is the H5 efficiency metric.

## What Engram does differently
AWM admits any workflow induced from a judged-success trajectory and never removes one. Engram makes each one a candidate, activates it after k of n replay passes on held-out tasks, tracks a smoothed success rate, and retires on drift or environment change (ADR 0011). AWM uses successes only; Engram feeds abandoned pi branches as negative evidence. Engram items also carry provenance and scope.

## Risk to our claims
Rule-based induction matches LM induction (Table 5) and about seven workflows carry the whole gain (Table 10). If the benefit is in having any workflows at all, a governed arm may not beat ungoverned AWM and H5 is a null. Mitigation: ungoverned AWM is its own condition in ADR 0015, and H5 is the paired delta, not the gain over no memory.

## Open question
There is no mechanism for evaluator false positives (Sec. 3.2.1). On pi coding sessions with no ground truth, what fraction of induced candidates would replay validation reject? Measure on a session fixture in step 0.
