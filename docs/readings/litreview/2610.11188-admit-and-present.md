## Claim
Memory governance at inference time splits into two levers, admission (which recalled units enter the working context) and presentation (how admitted units are rendered), and only admission moves the needle. Deterministic, typed admission policies cut judge-assessed failure on an external benchmark without retraining, but every variant also raises personalization misses, so selection quality buys safety at a utility cost the paper does not close.

## Method
Two inference-time designs sit between retrieval and the prompt. FC (factor-compiled admission) has an adjudicator model assign influence factors to each whole entry (scope, subject match, role, epistemic support, temporal status, utility); deterministic code maps the factors to five typed channels, with constraints and current preferences failing open and beliefs and unverified facts failing closed (Sec. 3.2). PS (permission-semantic admission) first splits entries into typed units so one entry can yield a permitted preference unit and a denied factual-claim unit; code maps unit attributes to five permitted uses plus deny, and denied units are physically absent from the prompt (Sec. 3.2). Two presentation renderings, PN (permission-note headers) and CH (channel headers), group the same admitted units (Sec. 3.3). Development surface is PersistBench (sycophancy n=122, beneficial use n=91, cross-domain leakage n=200) on Qwen3.7-Max, MiniMax-M3, Kimi-K2.6 and GPT-OSS-120B; the external benchmark is MemSyco-Bench, four tasks of 300 samples each, with DeepSeek-V4-Pro as generator and adjudicator; the judge is GLM-5.2 at temperature 0 (Sec. 4.1, 4.2). Baselines are bare verbatim injection and a reimplementation of the MemGate gate. The design is preregistered with gates on pooled effect and personalization loss (Sec. 5.5).

## Key numbers
- External pooled failure (n=1,199): Bare 46.2, Gate 44.1 (p=.08), FC 39.5 (p=2.7e-7), PS 37.4 (p=4.1e-12); PS vs FC not significant, p=.066 (Table 2, Sec. 5.2).
- Objective fact judgment failure: Bare 45.2, FC 23.7, PS 20.4; ofj answer accuracy 56.2 percent Bare to 75.9 percent PS, with a no-memory arm at 75.3 percent (Table 2, Sec. 5.2, 5.4).
- Personalized memory use failure rises under every design: Bare 14.7, Gate 22.7, FC 22.3, PS 20.0 (Table 2); FC +7.6pp, PS +5.3pp (Sec. 5.5).
- PS misses its own preregistered gate: 8.8pp pooled against a 10pp threshold, and minus 6.0pp pm accuracy against a 3pp limit (Sec. 5.5).
- Budget-matched: PS ofj failure 23.3 percent against 31.7 percent for random selection and 44.3 percent for relevance ranking at the same unit budget (Sec. 5.2).
- Cross-domain leakage, development surface, Bare to PS+PN: Qwen3.7-Max 44.0 to 14.5 percent; Kimi-K2.6 37.0 to 22.5; MiniMax-M3 9.5 to 4.0; GPT-OSS-120B 64.0 to 70.0, not significant (Table 1, Sec. 5.1).
- Tightening admission alone on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (n=200): 42.5 to 25.0 percent cross-domain failure, p=1.6e-4 (Sec. 5.4).
- Presentation PN vs CH pooled difference 0.8pp, 95 percent CI [-2.7, +1.0]; preregistered cross-backbone test 0 of 4 significant (Table 3, Sec. 5.3).
- Memory-evidence conflict residual: 82.3 to 85.0 percent misleading across all governed arms against 91.7 Bare and 0 percent no-memory (Sec. 5.4).
- Judge check: binary kappa 0.70 on n=352 development items; no human validation on external rubrics (Sec. 4.2).

## What Engram borrows
The admission versus presentation split is the cleanest published statement of what the `context` hook decides. The asymmetric fail-open/fail-closed rule by unit type lands in ADR 0006 as a write-rule dimension per (class, source kind): constraints and current preferences admitted by default, model-written beliefs denied until a lint passes. The typed-unit split (one entry, several units with different eligibility) is an argument for ADR 0002 to keep `class` fine enough that a preference and a factual claim are never one item. The budget-matched control (same unit count, random versus relevance versus policy) becomes a required comparison in ADR 0015 so H1 is not confounded by token budget. The preregistered gate on personalization loss is the model for the H2 stopping rule in ADR 0016.

## What Engram does differently
Their adjudicator is a model that labels factors; the decision is then deterministic. Engram's gate has no model in the path at all for H1's enforcement condition: scope, grants, provenance kind and validity are fields set at write time, and the read filter is a pure function (ADR 0006, 0007). They govern a single-principal personal assistant; Engram governs multi-principal scopes and logs every admission as an event so the personalization loss they report is attributable per rule, not per arm.

## Risk to our claims
Every governed arm raised personalization failure by 5 to 8pp and the authors could not design their way out of it (Table 2, Sec. 5.5). That is the governance tax H2 predicts, but it also shows the tax may not have a knee at all for utility-type memory: tighter admission monotonically loses beneficial use. Engram must report personalization-type loss separately from safety gains, and if lints (H3) cannot reinstate denied-but-useful units, H2 has to be stated per memory class.

## Open question
Their no-memory arm matches the best governed arm on fact judgment (75.3 vs 75.9 percent, Sec. 5.4). For which benchmark tasks does any memory beat no memory under governance, and should step 0 add "no memory" as the eighth condition rather than treating long-context as the only ungoverned baseline?
