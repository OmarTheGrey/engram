## Claim
Fusing a late-interaction dense score with BM25 under one weight, with no training, adds many points on LoCoMo but little on LongMemEval-S, where BM25 is already strong. An off-the-shelf cross-encoder reranker hurt in the one configuration tried.

## Method
Replicates Turn Isolation Retrieval (a session scores as its best turn), fuses it with BM25 under a leave-one-conversation-out weight, across six encoders, on LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S. Abstract only.

## Key numbers
- Fusion adds 8.8 to 17.2 points of LoCoMo Hit@1 over late interaction alone (all p<1e-4); e5-large-v2 reaches Hit@1 0.752 (abstract).
- A web-search cross-encoder on the fused top-10 lowers Hit@1 by 6.9 pp (abstract).
- LongMemEval-S: net gain over BM25 small and not significant (abstract).

## What Engram borrows
A linear fusion with one weight is easy to explain as two named components. A cross-encoder reranker is not worth a PR before a measured win here. Lands in AB-RET-2 and in deferring a reranker.

## What Engram does differently
Engram fixes the weight by policy and logs it, rather than fitting it per run.

## Risk to our claims
The gain depends on the corpus regime, so a hybrid tier must be reported per benchmark, not as a general improvement.

## Open question
On GateMem, where principals and scopes dominate, does relevance matter enough for fusion to show?
