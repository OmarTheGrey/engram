## Claim
Task success says nothing about memory quality, because a memoryless agent completes tasks just as well. Memory should be audited by how much of a hidden, structured user state can be recovered from what it stored, and how much of that is reachable through the system's own top-k retrieval rather than a full dump.

## Method
MemProbe (the benchmark; the title says MemAudit) builds 50 simulated users from DeepPersona profiles, each with a hidden bank of 31 taxonomy-anchored dimensions under five categories: Skill Memory, Knowledge Memory, Episodic Memory, Self Model and Assistance Preference, seeded from O*NET, Conway, McAdams and HEXACO (Sec. 3.2). Each dimension has a leak-controlled task on an adjacent topic that never names the target, enforced by a blind anti-fishing critic; 31 tasks per user, 25 turns each, memory reset between users (Sec. 3.3, 4.1). A slot-filling reader then reconstructs the bank under two access modes: dump_all returns the whole store, retrieve returns the top 5 from the system's own search (Sec. 3.1). An LLM judge scores each dimension on {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}; category-balanced recovery B is the unweighted mean of five category means (Sec. 3.4). Unrecovered targets are attributed to task design, memory failure, agent elicitation or simulator strictness (Sec. 3.5). Systems: nomem, longctx_full (verbatim turns), A-Mem, Mem0 and Mem-T-4B; evaluators use gpt-5.4-mini (Sec. 4.1).

## Key numbers
- Task completion 99.871 to 99.935 percent for every system including nomem (Table 2).
- B full-store: longctx_full 0.624, mem0 0.613, amem 0.611; B top-k: amem 0.540, longctx_full 0.503, mem0 0.473, memt 0.465 (Table 2). memt full-store 0.130 is diagnostic only because its store overflows the reader's context.
- Footprint: amem 109 items, longctx_full 155, mem0 204, memt 471 (Table 2).
- Episodic is the weakest category everywhere, 0.229 to 0.451; Preference the strongest, 0.720 to 0.773 (Table 3).
- Recovery rate at score at least 0.75: amem 65.52 percent dump_all, 57.09 top-k; mem0 66.05 and 47.76 (Table 4).
- Top-k costs 8 to 18 points on disclosed targets; task-design failures account for only 43 to 45 dimensions per system (Sec. 4.4).

## What Engram borrows
The dump_all versus retrieve split becomes a required reporting pair for every recall benchmark in ADR 0015: "written but not reachable" is a governance outcome, since a scope or grant decision that hides an item looks identical to a retrieval miss unless both modes are reported. The five-category taxonomy is a candidate schema for the twin's interview-bootstrapped person scope (ADR 0021), and the leak-controlled task is the template for twin probes. Saturated task success is why U in the GateMem arm is judged on factual coverage, not completion.

## What Engram does differently
MemProbe measures what a store retains about one user. Engram asks who may recover it: a twin's person scope should score high for its owner and zero for any other principal, so the audit becomes a pair of recovery numbers per (store, requester). Provenance per item also lets "memory failure" split into never-written (no write event) and written-then-retired (retire event with reason).

## Risk to our claims
Top-k alone loses 8 to 18 points of recovery. A governed read that filters by scope and grants before top-k adds a second filter, so H2's tax on recall may exceed the gate's cost alone. Engram must report B under the governed read with the owner as requester, measured against ungoverned top-k, not dump_all.

## Open question
Could the recovery audit serve as an erasure test for the twin: after a person-scope deletion report, recovery for that user should fall to nomem's 0.000. Is that a cheaper erasure certificate than GateMem's forgetting checkpoints?
