## Claim
An attacker who can only send queries and read outputs, like any ordinary user, can get an agent to write malicious reasoning records into a shared memory bank that later redirect other users' queries. No access to the store, the model or the victims' prompts is needed.

## Method
MINJA targets agents that store (query, reasoning) records and retrieve them by similarity. A malicious record pairs a benign-looking query containing a victim term with a bridging step ("data of A saved under B") followed by reasoning for the target term. The attacker induces the bridging step with an indication prompt appended to the query, then runs progressive shortening: each iteration drops one step of the prompt, relying on already-stored records to supply the missing reasoning, until the stored record contains no visible prompt (Algorithm 1, App. A). Attack queries are shuffled with benign ones; the agent writes the records itself through its normal feedback-gated storage. Evaluated on RAP on Webshop, EHRAgent on MIMIC-III and eICU, and a CoT QA agent on MMLU, with GPT-4 and GPT-4o and cosine top-k retrieval (Sec. 5.1). ISR is the share of attack queries that inject targeted reasoning, ASR the share of held-out victim queries whose responses contain it, UD the change on benign non-victim queries.

## Key numbers
- Overall ISR/ASR/UD (Table 1): EHR MIMIC-III GPT-4 95.6/57.0/-0.7; EHR eICU 98.5/90.0/0.0; RAP Webshop GPT-4 96.3/77.4/-1.5; RAP Webshop GPT-4o 99.3/98.9/-0.7; MMLU GPT-4 and GPT-4o 100.0/68.9/-10.0.
- Introduction summary: ISR 98.2 percent, ASR 76.8 percent.
- Prior poisoning degrades ISR and ASR by 6 to 20 points (Sec. 5.3, Table 2).
- Benign density: MIMIC-III ASR 68.9 at 25 benign queries, 31.1 at 100 (Table 4).
- Prompt-level detection with GPT-4o judge (Table 5): EHR-MIMIC targeted 131/135 caught but 34/50 false positives; RAP targeted 0/135; QA agent targeted 0/90.

## What Engram borrows
The attack is the canonical case for the `provenance.kind: model` rule in ADR 0004: a record the agent wrote from its own reasoning is untrusted by default and cannot land in a scope shared with other principals without a gate pass. The write invariant is exactly the control MINJA lacks, since every injected record is a model-authored episodic item written into a pool that serves everyone. The bridging-step records become a fault fixture (L2), and the benign-density result motivates the near-duplicate lint (ADR 0018) as a cheap partial defence. The Table 5 trade-off, precise prompts lacking generality and general prompts over-flagging, is cited in ADR 0008 as the reason model-based checks are lints, not gates.

## What Engram does differently
MINJA assumes the agent decides what to store and a shared bank with no owner. Engram gives every item an owner and scope, so a record written in user X's session lands in X's person scope and is never retrieved for user Y without a grant. The defence is structural; Engram does not try to recognise a bridging step.

## Risk to our claims
MINJA notes that identity disguise or account hijacking defeats isolated-memory designs. Engram assumes the harness authenticates the principal; if the actor field is wrong, the invariant holds on paper and fails in practice. The thesis must state that principal identity is an input, not something the plane establishes.

## Open question
In a role scope shared by design (a clinical team), MINJA-style records from one member are legitimately readable by others. Does the duplicate lint or an unsupported-claim lint catch a bridging step with no evidence ref, and at what false-positive rate on real team memory?
