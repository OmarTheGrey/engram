## Claim
Agent benchmarks that report accuracy alone reward needlessly expensive agents, conflate what model developers and application developers need, lack holdout sets, and are not reproducible; cost must be a first-class axis, and simple baselines often sit on the Pareto frontier.

## Method
The authors rerun published agent architectures on HumanEval (164 problems, five runs each) and plot accuracy against dollar cost: LATS, LDB, Reflexion, and three baselines they build (retry, warming, escalation) (Sec. 2.2 to 2.3, Figure 1, Table A1). They jointly optimize accuracy and cost on HotPotQA with DSPy (Sec. 3.2), show through NovelQA how asking all questions at once misstates RAG versus long-context cost (Sec. 4.1), survey 17 benchmarks for holdout adequacy against a four-level generality table (Sec. 5, Table 1), examine STeP on WebArena as overfitting (Sec. 5.1), and attempt to reproduce WebArena and HumanEval results (Sec. 6).

## Key numbers
- HumanEval: LDB (GPT-4) 93.3 percent at $6.36; warming (GPT-4) 93.2 at $2.45; GPT-4 zero-shot 89.6 at $1.93; LATS (GPT-4) 88.0 at $134.50; escalation 85.0 at $0.27 (Table A1).
- Warming shows no significant accuracy difference from the best architecture; LATS costs over 50 times more (Sec. 2.3).
- Joint optimization on HotPotQA cut variable cost 53 percent (GPT-3.5) and 41 percent (Llama-3-70B) at similar accuracy; fixed cost pays off after about 1,350 tasks (Sec. 3.2).
- WebArena: 812 tasks; STeP reports 35.8 percent with hardcoded task-specific policies (Sec. 5.1).
- Of 17 benchmarks, seven have no holdout and no plan to add one; fully general with unseen-domain holdouts, 0 of 2 (Sec. 5, Table 1).
- LATS and STeP marked some incorrect tasks correct and removed 1 and 8 tasks (Sec. 6).
- Cost variance over five runs: LATS (GPT-4) $123.98 to $147.13 (Table A1).

## What Engram borrows
Three rules. ADR 0017: every model call records input and output tokens so cost can be recomputed as prices change, and the H2 frontier is drawn from that ledger. ADR 0015: no-memory and long-context conditions are mandatory, because simple baselines land on the frontier and an unrun baseline invalidates any claim that governance earns its tax. ADR 0016: repeated runs with intervals become three seeds with paired bootstrap, and the stopping rule adds seeds rather than reporting one run. The generality table is used in S0.6 to classify each benchmark and state which level any H4 claim can reach.

## What Engram does differently
This paper treats the agent as the unit of cost. Engram meters the governance layer separately so the H2 knee is a cost of policy, not of architecture. The paper recommends secret holdouts; Engram pre-registers instead, because the benchmarks are public.

## Risk to our claims
STeP shows a method tuned on a public benchmark can double its score without generalising. Engram's policies are written by people who have read the benchmarks. If an enforced policy encodes benchmark structure, H1 and H4 are overfit. Mitigation: policies are frozen in the pre-registration before any run, and H4 includes a benchmark not used in policy design.

## Open question
Model developers should use compute proxies and application developers dollars, the paper says. Engram's three providers price differently; do we report the H2 knee in tokens, dollars, or both with the dollar figure dated?
