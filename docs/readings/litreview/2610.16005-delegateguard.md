## Claim
A reference monitor outside the model that turns the initiating user's authorisation into attenuable capabilities (purpose, labels, operations, sinks, delegation depth) blocks confused-deputy exfiltration, authority amplification and revocation replay in delegated agent workflows.

## Method
From the abstract: capabilities attenuate down the delegation graph, data-moving actions are mediated outside the LLM, revocation propagates through the graph, and a trace-level safety invariant is formalised. Evaluated against an adaptive adversary, four baselines, model-action and multi-turn studies, and checks on deterministic traces and 517,401 Enron emails. The full paper was not read.

## Key numbers
- Static allowlists dominated on the revocation and intra-tool cases, 10 of 10 (abstract page).
- Two open models proposed the attacker's goal action on 42 of 45 and 44 of 45 injected traces; all were blocked at execution (abstract page).
- Multi-turn study, 90 runs: leakage 0.844 down to 0.20 with full task completion (abstract page).

## What Engram borrows
The invariant shape: an agent acting for a person holds a subset of that person's rights, never more, and revoking the person's grant revokes the agent's at the next call. Engram has the pieces without a schema change: `Actor.memberships` is the attenuation vehicle, and ADR 0034 refuses grant changes by a model-sourced actor, the "an agent cannot widen its own authority" half. A delegation invariant (an eighth, candidate) checks both on a drawn delegation chain. Purpose and sink labels have no Engram analogue and stay out of scope.

## What Engram does differently
Engram governs memory reads and writes, not tool sinks, and holds no capability tokens; authority is the scope and grant state in the log at the call's time.

## Risk to our claims
Their premise that any holder which read memory is adversarial by default says a read filter alone is insufficient once recalled content reaches a sending tool. Engram's claim is limited to the memory boundary; state that limit in the thesis.
