## Claim
Content-judging defences leave most memory-poisoning attacks in place and whole-source refusal destroys recall. Labelling each claim with kind, authority and scope, and admitting it only when it meets what the request needs, blocks attacks while keeping legitimate memory.

## Method
Each claim carries a kind (ordered DESCRIPTION, ATTRIBUTE, PREFERENCE, RULE, PERMISSION), an authority (the strongest kind its source channel may establish; a principal's own turn gives PERMISSION, an external artifact only DESCRIPTION) and a scope (whom it covers). A use classifier reads only the request and sets the kind required. Retrieval admits a claim only if its kind and authority meet the required kind within scope, filtering before ranking. A derived-from graph caps a claim's authority at the lowest authority among its inputs. Evaluated on MPBench (3,241 attack and 2,999 benign cases), LoCoMo and EHRAgent under MINJA, on four backbones against A-MemGuard, DRIFT, RandEC, an LLM sanitiser, MemLineage and TMA-NM.

## Key numbers
- MPBench ASR falls from 26.7 to 2.6 percent on GPT-5.6-terra and from 49.8 to 6.2 percent on GPT-4o-mini (Table 1).
- Recall rises to 86.3 and 85.9 percent against 41.1 and 75.9 with no defence; TMA-NM drops recall to 9.6 percent for similar ASR (Table 1).
- EHRAgent under MINJA, GPT-4o-mini: the attack still generates wrong-patient code on 72.4 percent of victim questions under SAFE-Mem, but none of those calls executes because the call check refuses them; without the scope check 44.4 percent of attacks execute (Table 2 and text; the table was read from a flattened PDF, so column alignment is uncertain). Benign memories lost: 2.0 percent against 70.4 and 71.4 percent for TMA-NM and isolation.
- 97 and 91 percent of MPBench attack queries need a kind above description (Sec. 6.1).

## What Engram borrows
Three ideas the litreview already points at, now concrete: (1) derived items inherit the lowest authority of their inputs, the compaction trust ceiling noted in sota-2026.md; (2) authority comes from the write-time channel, as in Origin-Bound Authority; (3) a kind lattice (description below permission) as an admission rule on the read side. Deriving the required kind from the request alone keeps memory content from steering its own admission.

## What Engram does differently
SAFE-Mem classifies kind with an LLM and sits in one store. Engram's kinds come from the system at write, the gate is deterministic across stores, and each decision is an event.

## Risk to our claims
It reports a defence with higher recall than no defence, which weakens any statement that governance has an unavoidable tax. H2 should report SAFE-Mem style admission as a baseline or explain why Engram's presets differ.

## Open question
Is a five-level kind lattice expressible with Engram's existing trust kinds, or does it need a schema change (rule 5)?
