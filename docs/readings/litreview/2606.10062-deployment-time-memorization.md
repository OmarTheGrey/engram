## Claim
Memory design is a deployment-time privacy choice. Summarising reduces extraction risk while keeping most personalisation, but deletion that purges only raw data leaves recoverable copies in derived tiers.

## Method
On LongMemEval with canary strings, the authors sweep summarisation aggressiveness, retrieval breadth k and deletion mode (raw-only, full-pipeline purge, tombstone redaction). Metrics: Personalization Recall, Adversarial Extraction Rate, and a Forgetting Residue Score (FRS) over memory tiers.

## Key numbers
- Key-fact summarisation cut canary extraction by 76 percent (Gemma 3 12B) and 64 percent (GPT-4o-mini) with nearly all recall kept (abstract page).
- Raw-only deletion left summary copies recoverable in about 20 percent of instances.
- Only full-pipeline purge or tombstone redaction brought worst-tier residue to zero.

## What Engram borrows
FRS as a metric for the erasure acceptance test: after offboard, residue across projection, explanation rows, log events, store and derived items must be zero (card AB-TWN-1, and a column in the AB-TWN-4 report).

## What Engram does differently
Engram's erase already covers projection, explanation rows, log redaction and store; its sweep already deletes store records whose lineage names an erased id, but promotions out of the person scope stay by the lead's 2026-11-20 decision 38a, and Distill procedures mined from the person's sessions are not yet covered.

## Risk to our claims
Distill skills mined from a leaver's sessions are a derived tier the offboarding report does not mention.

## Open question
Should FRS be an eighth invariant checked in make test-invariants?
