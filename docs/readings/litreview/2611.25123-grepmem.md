## Claim
An append-only plain-text log of the full history plus a regular-expression search tool is enough as agent memory, and beats lossy summarisation and masking on tasks that overflow the context window.

## Method
GrepMem keeps the agent's complete history in an append-only text log and gives the agent a regex search tool, with no embeddings, index, extraction or extra models. Qwen3.5-9B is trained with reinforcement learning under five context-management strategies and evaluated on five tasks including ContextBench and BCP-Multi.

## Key numbers
- 95 to 97 percent accuracy on ContextBench against at most 21 percent for lossy baselines (abstract).
- 8.8 points above SUPO's summarisation on BCP-Multi (abstract).
- On the accuracy and token-cost Pareto frontier across all five tasks (abstract).
- No comparison to full-context models in the abstract.

## What Engram borrows
Engram's event log is already append-only and lossless, so a "grep over the log" read tool is a cheap store to put under the plane: the files adapter plus a regex recall, giving H4 a fourth, trivially simple store with no model call.

## What Engram does differently
GrepMem has no scope, grants or validity: a grep reads everything. A governed version must filter by scope before matching, which is a read-filter test case.

## Risk to our claims
Evidence that simple lossless stores beat extraction-based ones weakens the case that stores need elaborate memory systems; it does not affect governance claims, but H4 should include a lossless baseline store.

## Open question
Does regex recall under the read filter lose recall when scope filters remove matches?
