## Claim
An agent whose memory is a person's own two-hour interview transcript predicts that person's survey answers and personality about as well as the person predicts themselves two weeks later, beats agents built from demographics or a written persona, and narrows accuracy gaps across political and racial groups.

## Method
A stratified U.S. sample of 1,052 participants each completed a two-hour voice interview with an AI interviewer following the American Voices Project protocol (Sec. 2). Three agent types per participant: interview-only, survey-only (General Social Survey items plus BFI-44), and survey-plus-interview; all use GPT-4o, with "expert reflections" generated by four simulated social-science personas over the source material. Baselines: a demographic agent and a persona agent built from a short description. Evaluation: held-out GSS items, Big Five, five economic games, and five experimental replications. Every score is normalized by the participant's own two-week test-retest consistency, so 1.0 means the agent matches the person as well as the person matches themselves (Sec. 3).

## Key numbers
- GSS normalized accuracy: interview 0.83, survey 0.82, survey-plus-interview 0.86; demographic 0.74; persona 0.71 (Results, p. 6; abstract gives 83, 82, 86 and 74 percent).
- Big Five: interview agents reach normalized correlation 0.80 (raw r = 0.78); demographic 0.61; persona 0.75 (Results, p. 7).
- Economic games: no significant difference among agent types, F(4, 5255) = 1.63, p = 0.16 (Results, p. 7).
- Experimental replications: agent-predicted effect sizes correlate with human effect sizes at r = 0.91 to 0.99; the authors call the five-study design underpowered (Table 1, p. 8).
- Bias: Demographic Parity Difference on GSS political ideology falls from 13.75 percent (demographic) to 8.60 (interview), 6.22 (survey), 7.09 (combined); on Big Five from 0.166 to 0.063 (Figure 3, pp. 9 to 10).
- Consent allows sharing with researchers for academic purposes only; removal requests are honoured for 25 years (Methods).

## What Engram borrows
Twin bootstrapping from an interview is a prompt template in the pi package (ADR 0021, E9.1), and this paper is the evidence that an interview is the right seed rather than a profile form. The test-retest normalization becomes the twin's recall metric: a person-scope recall is scored against what the person said again later, not against an absolute label (E9.4). The DPD check is adopted as a fairness report on twin recall across groups. The 25-year removal commitment is the reference point for person-scope erasure with a deletion report (ADR 0010).

## What Engram does differently
This paper builds simulators that answer as the person. Engram's twin is a scoped memory that answers about the person to whoever holds a grant, and never without one. The interview is a memory source with provenance, not a persona prompt, and the role scope hands over by event. Engram does not predict behaviour and makes no claim about survey accuracy.

## Risk to our claims
Interview grounding beats demographics by 0.09 on GSS and nothing at all on economic games. If a twin's value for a successor is mostly in facts a short profile would capture, the person-scope machinery is expensive relative to a role handover document, and H4's cost side suffers. Mitigation: E9.5 compares successor task performance with the twin against a one-page handover note as its baseline.

## Open question
The authors do not know why self-report grounding works so well (Discussion). For Engram the practical version is: which parts of an interview transcript does a successor actually retrieve? The explanation log (ADR 0009) can answer this on the dogfood twins without any new experiment.
