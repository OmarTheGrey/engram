## Claim
Believable long-running agents need three things on top of an LLM: a memory stream of natural-language records, scored retrieval, and periodic reflection that writes higher-level memories which cite their evidence. Ablating any one of them measurably lowers believability.

## Method
Twenty-five agents in a Sims-style town run on gpt-3.5-turbo (Section 4). Every observation is a memory object with text, creation time and last-access time (4.1). Retrieval scores each memory on recency (exponential decay, factor 0.995 per game hour), importance (LLM-rated 1 to 10 at write time) and relevance (embedding cosine), min-max normalised and summed with all weights 1 (4.1). Reflection triggers when summed importance of recent events exceeds 150; the LLM asks three questions over the 100 most recent memories, retrieves, and writes five insights per question, each with pointers to cited memories (4.2). Plans live in the stream and are regenerated on reaction (4.3). Evaluation: 100 Prolific raters rank interview answers across five conditions with TrueSkill and Kruskal-Wallis (Section 6); a two-day run measures diffusion and coordination (Section 7).

## Key numbers
- TrueSkill mu, Figure 8 and Section 6.5.1: full architecture 29.89, no reflection 26.88, no reflection or planning 25.64, crowdworker 22.95, fully ablated 21.21. Kruskal-Wallis H(4) = 150.29, p < 0.001.
- Diffusion, Section 7.1.2: Sam's candidacy known by 1 of 25 agents at start and 8 (32 percent) at end; the party by 1 at start and 13 (52 percent) at end.
- Hallucination check, Section 7.1.2: 6 of 453 responses (1.3 percent) hallucinated.
- Network density, Section 7.1.2: 0.167 to 0.74.
- Coordination, Section 7.1.2: 12 agents invited, 5 attended, 3 cited conflicts, 4 expressed interest but did not plan to come.
- Cost, Section 8.2: "thousands of dollars in token credits" for 25 agents over two days.

## What Engram borrows
- The three retrieval score components are the first fields of the `explanation` record in ADR 0009: recency, importance, relevance, each reported separately with the rank.
- Reflections that point to their evidence are the model for `provenance` on model-written items (ADR 0004, `kind: model`, with `session_entry_id` references) and for the unsupported-claim lint: a semantic item whose cited evidence is absent or retired is a finding.
- Section 8.3's call for audit logs against misuse is a citation for the event log as a product requirement, not only a research instrument.
- The five-area interview protocol is a template for Twin evaluation (ADR 0021).

## What Engram does differently
Generative Agents has one owner per stream and no policy: everything observed is written, everything scored is readable. Engram inserts a gate before the write, a filter and explanation before the read, and a lint pass over what was stored. Reflections here are never re-validated; Engram's candidate status and replay admission (ADR 0011) exist because reflections drift.

## Risk to our claims
Section 8.2 names vulnerability to "memory hacking" and the ablation shows that reflection, a model-written write, is worth about 3 TrueSkill points. If the gate or the `kind: model` trust rule blocks or demotes reflections, H2's governance tax could land on exactly the writes that carry the believability gain. We would run a Twin arm with reflection allowed at person scope only and measure that tax directly.

## Open question
Reflection runs when summed importance exceeds 150, a store-side trigger with no policy. Should Engram expose a "consolidation budget" per scope so the plane, not the agent, decides when model-written summaries may be produced?
