## Claim
Content-aware chunking clearly beats fixed-length splitting for dense retrieval, and the best strategy depends on the domain.

## Method
36 segmentation methods across six knowledge domains and five embedding models, scored by an LLM grader with graded relevance, main metric nDCG@5, plus Hit@5 and MRR. Retrieval only, no generation. Abstract page read, not the full paper.

## Key numbers
- Paragraph Group Chunking: mean nDCG@5 about 0.459, Precision@1 about 24%, Hit@5 about 59% (abstract page).
- Fixed-size character chunking: nDCG@5 below 0.244, Precision@1 about 2 to 3% (abstract page).
- Dynamic token sizing best in biology, physics, health; paragraph grouping best in legal and maths (abstract page).

## What Engram borrows
A default chunker that respects structure (paragraph groups, heading paths) and a recorded chunker id and version in each ingest event, so a re-chunk is a governed new version. Lands in AB-ING-1.

## What Engram does differently
Chunk boundaries here are provenance spans, not only retrieval units: each chunk keeps its selector into the source, so a recall can cite the exact span.

## Risk to our claims
The ranking is on retrieval quality with an LLM judge, not on citation faithfulness. Do not cite it for span accuracy.

## Open question
Does the chunker choice change source-changed noise, that is, one small edit invalidating many chunks?
