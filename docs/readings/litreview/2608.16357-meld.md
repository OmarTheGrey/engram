## Claim
An incoming claim to an agent memory graph is decided as insert, merge, relate, conflict or reject from three signals (claim-key identity, embedding similarity, an NLI verdict), applied as one auditable Patch. Contradictions are preserved for adjudication, not auto-resolved.

## Method
A merge classifier over claim pairs and a per-claim status CRDT, evaluated on HotpotQA distractor and a real edge-to-HPC deployment. Abstract only.

## Key numbers
- Merge classifier AUC 0.968; false-merge rate 0.013; reconvergence 30 of 30 partition-heal trials against 11 of 30 for last-writer-wins; about 11 percent less live storage (abstract).
- A search snippet reported macro-F1 0.765 to 0.845 across encoders; not verified in the paper.

## What Engram borrows
The five-way decision vocabulary and the rule that a conflict is recorded, never silently resolved, which matches lint-as-event. An NLI verdict as a third signal beside the entity key and similarity.

## What Engram does differently
Engram records a verdict as a lint event with a reason and leaves supersession to a governed write.

## Risk to our claims
The paper's own named hazard is the NLI model missing a real contradiction so a merge goes through silently. A local NLI lint must be reported with recall, not only precision.

## Open question
Which NLI model do they use, and at what threshold?
